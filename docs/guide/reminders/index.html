Reminders

Reminders as lists or boards, habits, and logs for your home and car.

Adding a reminder

Write down what to remember, with a day and a time if you want them.

How do I add one?

  • On the Mac, on Today or in a list, press ⌘N, type what to remember in New Reminder, and press Return.
  • On the iPhone, type what to remember in New Reminder, at the bottom of Today or of a list, and press Return.

While you type, Day and Time show above it, and give it a day and a time. On Today the day is already today. A time needs a day. A reminder with no day waits in its list and is never in Today.

Changing one

  • On the Mac, click a reminder to see it in the inspector, and change its title, notes, day, time or list there.
  • On the iPhone, tap a reminder to change its title, notes, day, time or list.

Today, and marking done

What is due now, from every list, and how to tick it off.

Today shows what is overdue and what is due today. A reminder due today stays there all day, even after its time has passed.

Marking done

  • On the Mac, the circle beside a reminder marks it done. So does ⇧⌘C, for the one selected. Mark Not Done puts it back.
  • On the iPhone, the circle beside a reminder marks it done. Mark Not Done, on its own screen, puts it back.

Nothing changes by itself. An overdue reminder waits until you mark it done or give it another day.

Lists

Keep reminders in lists, and move them between lists.

Every reminder is in a list. You start with one, To Do.

Adding and moving

  • On the Mac, New List adds another, and a reminder moves to another list with List, in the inspector.
  • On the iPhone, New List, on Lists, adds another, and a reminder moves to another list with List, on its own screen.

Deleting a list

Deleting a list sends it to Recently Deleted with the reminders in it. Choosing it there brings them back together. The last list can’t be deleted, because there must always be one to add to.

Alerts for reminders

Done and Snooze, right on the alert.

A reminder with a day and a time alerts at that time, on this device’s clock. The first time you give a reminder a time, the system asks whether the app may alert you, unless it has asked already.

The buttons

An alert has two buttons: Done, and Snooze for 10 Minutes. Done marks the reminder done, even while the app is locked. Snooze brings the alert back ten minutes later.

Two choices

Settings ▸ Alerts: Reminders has two choices. With “Show the words in alerts” off, a reminder’s alert says just “Reminder”. With “Alert for reminders with only a day” on, a reminder with a day and no time alerts on its day at the time you choose.

How many are set

The soonest alerts are set first. Opening the app sets the later ones as their turn comes. An alert is set on your device and rings from it. Nothing is sent to bring it.

More for reminders

The switches that add repeats, typed dates, flags and early alerts.

Settings ▸ Features: Reminders turns on more: Repeating, Typed Dates, Flags, Early Alerts, Habits, Maintenance Logs and Boards. Each is off until you turn it on. On the Mac, Features at the foot of the sidebar shows them.

Repeating

A repeating reminder makes its next one when you mark this one done, and not before.

Turning a switch off

Turning a feature off hides what it adds and changes nothing you entered. A reminder that already repeats keeps repeating.

Tags in reminders

On the Mac: group reminders by a word, and start lists from a template.

Tags

Turn on Tags in Settings ▸ Features: Reminders, then write a word with # in front of it in a reminder’s title or notes, such as #errands. The sidebar lists every tag with how many reminders still to do hold it. Choose one to see them, from every list.

Capitals and accents don’t matter, and nothing is added to a reminder for its tags: they are read from its words. A reminder marked done leaves its tags’ counts.

Templates

Turn on Templates, and any list whose name holds #template is a template. Reminder ▸ New List from Template… (⌥⌘N) makes a new list with its reminders still to do, with their notes and without their days. The template stays as it is, and one Undo takes the new list back.

Saved views in your reminders

On the Mac: keep a list or a tag as a named view in the sidebar.

How do I save one?

  1. In Settings ▸ Features: Reminders, turn on Saved Views.
  2. Choose a list in the sidebar, or, with Tags on, a tag. Saving a tag, choose Every List, or one list to see only its reminders.
  3. Choose Reminder ▸ Save This View… (⌥⌘S).

Using your views

Your views are in the sidebar, under Saved Views. Choose one to see its list, or the reminders that hold its tag. Control-click a view to rename it, move it up or down, or delete it, or drag it to another place.

A deleted view waits in Recently Deleted. Export the Saved Views as CSV…, in Settings ▸ Export: Reminders, writes them as a file.

Habits

Check off what you want to do regularly, and look back at how it’s going.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Reminders, turn on Habits. Habits is then a place in the Reminders: above your lists in the Mac’s sidebar, and as a row at the head of the iPhone’s lists.

Making a habit

  • On the Mac, choose Reminder ▸ New Habit….
  • On the iPhone, in Habits, tap +.

Give it a name and how often: every day, on some days of the week, or so many times a week, from one to six. Turn on Remind Me, and it reminds you at that time on each day it is due and not yet done.

Marking it

The circle beside a habit marks it done today, and tapping it again takes that back. A habit can be marked on any day. A square in the grid of the last twelve weeks marks that day.

What it shows

  • Plain counts: how many of the last 30 days, or how many this week, such as “3 of 4 this week”.
  • From two in a row, its streak, such as “5 days in a row”: in days for a daily habit, in weeks for one with so many times a week, and in due days for one on some days. A day not yet done today doesn’t end a streak; a day missed does.
  • A grid of the past twelve weeks, a column a week. Each square is done, missed (a day it was due and wasn’t done), not due, or still to come.

The reminder

A habit’s reminder comes on each day it is due and not yet done, for the next eight days. It says the habit’s name, or just “Reminder” with the alert words hidden. It has no Done or Snooze buttons.

Today and deleting

On the Mac, on Today, the habits due today and not yet done are with the reminders, each with its circle. A deleted habit waits in Recently Deleted with its days.

Home and car maintenance logs

Write down what was done to your home or your car, and see what is due next.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Reminders, turn on Maintenance Logs. A group called Logs appears under your lists, one row for each log. On the iPhone it is a row, Logs, at the head of the lists.

Logs and entries

  • New Log makes a log for a car, the house or the furnace, read by miles, kilometers, hours or nothing.
  • Log Something adds an entry: what was done, the day, its cost and the reading. A log says what its entries cost this year and in all.

Recurring items

Add Recurring Item… says how often something is due: every so many months, every so many miles, or both, whichever comes first. For example, “Oil change: every 6 months or 5,000 miles”. Log It…, beside an item, logs it.

What is due next

Stonefold works it out. By time, from the last entry logged for the item, or the day the item was made. By reading, from the last entry with a reading, plus the step. With both, whichever comes first. A due day by the reading is worked out from the log’s readings once two are at least a month apart.

A reminder, if you want one

Choose a list under Remind Me In, and the item’s next due is a reminder there, such as “Oil change · Car”, moved each time you log it. When a reading counts, its notes say “Or at 45,000 miles”. Because it is an ordinary reminder, it shows on Today, on the calendar with Reminders on the Calendar, and in your alerts, with Done and Snooze.

Deleting

A deleted log waits in Recently Deleted with its entries and items, and comes back with them.

A list as a board

See a reminders list as cards in columns.

How do I turn it on?

  • On the Mac, turn on Boards in Settings ▸ Features: Reminders, choose a list, then Reminder ▸ View as Board (⌥⌘B).
  • On the iPhone, turn on Boards in Settings ▸ Features: Reminders, open a list, and choose View as Board in its menu.

Its reminders are cards in columns: To Do, Doing and Done to start.

Moving cards

  • On the Mac, drag a card to another column, or choose it and press ⌥⌘[ or ⌥⌘] to move it left or right. The arrow keys move through the cards, and Space marks the one chosen done.
  • On the iPhone, touch and hold a card, then drag it to another column. Tap a card to open its reminder.

Done means done: a card moved into Done is marked done, and one moved out of it is not done again.

Columns and cards

A column’s menu renames it, moves it, or deletes it, and Add Column… adds one before Done. A card shows its day and, when its notes hold lines that begin “- [ ]” or “- [x]”, how many of them are ticked.

Copying from Apple’s Reminders

Bring the lists you already have into Stonefold, once, when you choose to.

How do I do it?

  1. Open Settings ▸ Bring Mine Over: Reminders and choose Bring Mine Over….
  2. It reads the lists and reminders that Apple’s Reminders holds on this device, and says what would come.
  3. Choose Copy. Nothing is copied until you do.

The first time, the system asks whether the app may use your reminders. Answer no, and nothing is read or copied. The app then says where to allow it.

What comes over

Each list comes over as a new list, after yours, with its titles, notes, days and times, the repeats this app can follow and the early alerts it has. Done reminders stay behind, and so do locations, pictures, tags, flags and priorities.

Afterwards

It runs once, and nothing stays linked. Nothing in Apple’s Reminders is changed, and a change made there afterwards does not show here.

Deleted reminders

Deleted reminders wait in Recently Deleted until you empty it.

A deleted reminder waits in Recently Deleted until you empty it. Nothing is removed after a number of days. Choose it there to put it back as it was.

Wiping

Delete Now wipes one thing, and Empty Recently Deleted wipes everything there. Each asks first. What is wiped can’t be brought back. Backups made before then still hold it until they age out.
