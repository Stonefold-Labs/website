In development, for iPhone and Mac

Stonefold keeps your household’s calendar, reminders, notes and budget.

It’s one app for iPhone and Mac. What you put in it is stored on your devices, encrypted. There’s no account and no sign-up, and we can’t see what’s in it.

See what it does How it’s kept private

Why we made it this way

These are the beliefs behind Stonefold, each with what the app does about it.

  • Because you should only pay once.

    A budget app shouldn’t be a subscription. Stonefold has no subscription and no in-app purchases, so there’s nothing to renew.

  • Because you should own your data.

    What you keep is stored in encrypted files on your own devices, each with its own key kept there. We hold no copy of it and no key to it.

  • Because you should be able to leave, and take your data with you.

    Your events, reminders, notes and budget can be exported in formats other apps open, and your budget as a working spreadsheet.

  • Because a third party shouldn’t have your data.

    There’s no account and no server of ours for the app to talk to. It has no ads, no tracking and no analytics, and it uses no outside service. We can’t see what’s in it.

  • Because your life shouldn’t be spread across several apps.

    Calendar, Reminders, Notes and Budget are one app with one unlock. Bills from your budget can show up on the calendar and in Reminders, too.

  • Because you should have the freedom to customize and make it yours.

    Choose from eight themes and four typefaces. Keep only the sections you want, and turn on only the features you want. They stay off until you do.

  • Because a number should show its work.

    Every figure in the budget can show how it was worked out. What Stonefold works out is arithmetic or a plain rule, and it has no AI in it.

  • Because you shouldn’t have to take our word for it.

    In Settings, a page called What Leaves This Device says, section by section and in plain sentences, what stays on your device, what goes somewhere only because you saved it there, and what Apple can see.

Four sections, one app

Stonefold has four sections: Calendar, Reminders, Notes and Budget. When you first open it, it asks which you’d like to keep, and you can change your mind later in Settings. Each section starts small. Anything more is a feature you turn on yourself.

The pictures on this page are drawings for now. Pictures of the app itself will take their place when it’s finished.

Calendar

Calendar holds a household’s dates: birthdays, appointments, and who’s away and when. You see the month, with today circled, and each day’s events in order.

  • Month and day

    The month shows where the busy days are. Choose a day to see its events in order.

  • Plain events

    An event has a title, a place and notes. It lasts all day, or it starts and ends at times you set.

  • Times that stay put

    An event at 9:00 stays at 9:00 wherever your device is. With Time Zones turned on, you can fix an event to a place’s own time, which helps when you travel.

  • Features you choose

    Repeating events, alerts, more calendars, a week view and time zones are each a switch in Settings. When you change one day of a repeating event, Stonefold asks whether you mean only that one or that one and all the later ones.

Reminders

Reminders keeps lists of things to do, with alerts. It starts with one list, called To Do, and you can add as many more as you like. A reminder can have a day, or a day and a time.

  • Today

    Today shows what’s overdue and what’s due today, from all of your lists.

  • Repeats that wait for you

    A repeating reminder moves on only when you mark it done. An overdue reminder never changes by itself.

  • Features you choose

    Repeating, typed dates, flags and early alerts are off until you turn them on. A repeat can be as plain as every week or as particular as the second Tuesday of the month. With typed dates on, you can write “tomorrow 5pm” in a title and Stonefold reads it by plain rules.

  • Alerts with two buttons

    A reminder’s alert has two buttons, Done and Snooze for 10 Minutes, so you can deal with it without opening the app.

Notes

Notes is for plain notes. A new one is a tap away on the iPhone, or Command-N on the Mac, and you start typing. The first line is the title.

  • Pinned, then recent

    Pinned notes come first, then the notes you changed most recently.

  • Search every word

    Search looks through every word of every note as you type, and highlights what it found. The search index is kept inside the notes’ encrypted file.

  • Features you choose

    Folders, checklists and simple formatting are off until you turn them on. Formatting is kept as plain Markdown marks in the text, so a note stays readable anywhere.

  • Links only when you ask

    A link in a note opens in your browser only when you ask. Stonefold fetches no previews and no pictures from the web.

Budget

Budget is a household budget that you type by hand. It never connects to a bank, so there’s no bank login to hand over. Nothing is imported and nothing posts by itself.

  • Where you stand

    Home gives three answers: what’s safe to spend and about how much a day, whether the bills are covered, and whether you’re on pace for the month. Every figure can show how it was worked out, because it’s arithmetic and not AI.

  • Can I afford it

    Type an amount and Stonefold says yes, yes but from other everyday money, or not without touching bill money.

  • Quick to add

    Adding a purchase starts with the amount. Then comes who you paid, suggested as you type, and the category, which shows what it has left.

  • Bills and paydays ask

    On its due day a bill asks to be confirmed, with Paid, Different Amount or Not Yet. A payday asks the same way.

  • A plan and charts

    There’s a plan for each month, which you can change any month, and charts of where the money went. A made-up sample budget lets you look around before you start your own.

  • A spreadsheet to keep

    Your budget exports as a working spreadsheet, with your own figures and its formulas in place. If you stop using the app, you can carry on in the spreadsheet.

How the sections work together

Each section works by itself. Two switches in Settings let the sections show each other’s things, and both start off.

  • Bills on the calendar

    Each bill from the budget shows on its due day. The month marks the day with a small diamond. The day’s list shows the bill’s name, its amount and whether it’s paid, due or overdue.

  • Bills in Reminders

    The bills that are waiting sit at the head of Today, each with Paid, Different Amount and Not Yet. Choosing Paid records the payment in the budget.

  • Reminders on the calendar

    A reminder with a day shows on that day, under the day’s events, with a ring beside it. You can mark it done from there.

A bill is never copied out of the budget. The calendar and the reminders read it while the budget is open and keep it only in memory, so no name, no amount and no due day is written into their file.

Encryption, privacy and security

Everything you put into Stonefold is stored on your devices, encrypted. There’s no account, and there are no ads, no tracking and no analytics. We run no server for the app to talk to, and we can’t see what’s in it.

  • Three encrypted files

    What you keep is in three encrypted files on your device. One holds the calendar and the reminders, one holds the notes, and one holds the budget. Each file has its own random key, kept on your device. Nothing you type into the notes or the budget is written into the calendar and reminders’ file.

  • One unlock

    One unlock opens all three: Face ID or Touch ID, or your device’s passcode or password when that fails. By default, anyone who can unlock your device can open the app. If you want more than that, you can lock the budget, the notes or both with a passphrase of your own, which is never stored.

  • What locking does

    When the app locks, the notes and the budget are closed and their keys are forgotten, and the lock screen shows no words and no numbers. The calendar and the reminders are locked on screen, and the app can still read them in the background to set your alerts.

  • Nothing of ours to run

    There’s no account and no sign-up. We run nothing that the app talks to, so there’s no server of ours holding anything of yours. The app works without the internet.

  • No ads, tracking or AI

    Stonefold has no ads, no tracking and no analytics, and it uses no outside service of any kind. There’s no AI in it. What it works out is arithmetic or a plain rule, and it can show how it got there.

  • Alerts that say little

    Alerts are set on your device and ring from it. They don’t pass through anyone’s push servers. One setting makes the alert for a reminder or an event say only “Reminder” or “Event” in place of its title. A bill reminder never shows a name or an amount.

  • The recovery sheet

    The recovery sheet is one page you print, with a code for each file. It’s the way back in if a device’s keys are lost, for example on a new iPhone. Stonefold never stores a code and never writes the page to a file. Keep the sheet where you would keep a key. If you lose every device and the sheet, nobody can open what you kept, and that includes us. That is the price of the privacy.

  • A page that tells you

    In Settings there’s a page named What Leaves This Device. It says in plain sentences, section by section, what stays on your device, what goes somewhere only because you saved it there, and what Apple can see.

  • We can’t see what’s in it

    The app sends us nothing. We hold no copy of what you keep and no key to it.

What you keep can leave with you

Each of the three files is backed up automatically on your device, and each backup is encrypted with that file’s own key. Save a Copy makes a copy sealed with a passphrase you type for that copy, and it goes where you save it.

Exports come in formats that other software opens: a standard calendar file, a CSV file of your reminders, a folder of plain text notes and a working spreadsheet of your budget. Those files aren’t encrypted, so Stonefold asks first every time.
