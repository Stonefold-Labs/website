Clock and calculator

Timers, alarms, a stopwatch, countdowns, a focus timer, and calculators for sums, loans and units.

The stopwatch

Time something, mark laps, and see which was quickest.

How do I use it?

  • On the Mac, in Stopwatch, choose Start, or press ⌘Return from anywhere in the Clock. Lap (⌘L) marks a lap while it runs. Stop holds it, Go On carries on, and the last button (⌘R) clears it once it is stopped.
  • On the iPhone, in Stopwatch, tap Start. Lap marks a lap while it runs. Stop holds it, Go On carries on, and the last button clears it once it is stopped.

The laps

The laps are listed newest first, each with its own length. The quickest and the slowest are named once there are two.

While the app is closed

The stopwatch keeps the moment it started, not a count, so it keeps time while the app is closed or locked.

Timers

Run as many timers as you like, side by side.

How do I set one?

  • On the Mac, choose New Timer… (⌘N), set the hours, minutes and seconds or choose one of the lengths under them, give it a name if you like, and press Return.
  • On the iPhone, tap New Timer, set the hours, minutes and seconds or choose one of the lengths under them, give it a name if you like, and tap Start Timer.

A timer can be from 1 second to 99 hours 59 minutes 59 seconds. The ready-made lengths are 1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 minutes.

Running timers

Several can run at once. Each shows what is left and the time it rings. Pause holds it, Go On carries on, and Cancel puts it back to its whole length. A timer stays in the list to start again until you delete it.

A running timer survives the app closing: it keeps the moment it ends.

Alarms

Set an alarm once or on the days you choose.

How do I set one?

  • On the Mac, choose New Alarm… (⇧⌘N), pick the time, the days it repeats on and a label, and choose Save.
  • On the iPhone, tap New Alarm, pick the time, the days it repeats on and a label, and tap Save.

With no days chosen it rings once, at the next time it comes, and then reads as off. An alarm’s switch turns it off and on. Click or tap it to change it.

What it says

When an alarm or a timer rings it says only “Alarm” or “Timer”, never its label or name, because what it says shows on the Lock Screen.

How it rings

  • On the Mac, an alarm rings as a notification with a sound, while notifications for the app are allowed in System Settings ▸ Notifications. The system keeps 64 alerts for one app, and the soonest of the alarms, timers, reminders, events and bills are the ones set.
  • On the iPhone, the app asks to set alarms when you set your first. Allowed, alarms and timers ring through silent mode and a Focus, as an alarm clock does. Not allowed, they ring as notifications instead.

The world clock

See the time in other cities beside your own.

  • On the Mac, choose Add City… (⌥⌘N), type part of a city’s name, and choose it.
  • On the iPhone, tap Add City, type part of a city’s name, and choose it.

Each city shows its time, whether it is today, yesterday or tomorrow there, and how far ahead or behind it is.

The cities and their clock changes come from this device’s own list of time zones. Nothing is looked up anywhere.

Countdowns

Count the days to a date, once or every year.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Clock, turn on Countdowns. Countdowns is then a place in the Clock.

Making one

  • On the Mac, choose New Countdown… (⌥⇧⌘N).
  • On the iPhone, tap New Countdown. Tap one to change it.

Give it a name and a day, and a time if it has one. Every Year counts to the same day next year once it comes, as a birthday does. A countdown to 29 February comes on 28 February in other years.

What it says

Each says the days until it: “in 12 days”. Within two days it says the hours as well, and on the day itself with no time it says “Today”. One that has passed moves to Passed and says how long ago it was. Nothing is deleted by itself.

On the Mac, the countdowns to come are also on Today, and the soonest is on the status line.

The focus timer

Work in stretches with breaks, and see how many you finished today.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Clock, turn on Focus Timer. Focus is then a place in the Clock, after Timers.

How does it run?

Choose Start Focus (on the Mac, ⌥⌘Return). A focus runs, then a short break, again and again, with a long break after the last focus of the set. Then it rests until you start it again.

  • Pause holds it, and Go On carries on.
  • Skip Break goes straight to the next focus, or, from the long break, ends the set.
  • Stop Focus puts it away.

It starts at 25 minutes of focus, 5 of break and a long break of 15 after every fourth. Lengths… changes them while no focus is going. A focus can be 1 to 120 minutes, a break 1 to 60, and a long break can come after every 2 to 8 focuses.

What it rings and counts

It rings at the end of each focus and each break, as a timer rings, saying only “Focus done” or “Break done”. Each focus that ran its whole length counts toward today’s sessions. It shows that count in plain words. It keeps time while the app is closed.

The calculator

A small calculator that keeps nothing of yours.

Where is it?

  • On the Mac, View ▸ Calculator (⌃⌘C) opens a small window of its own. The command box finds it too. Locked, it shows only “Locked”.
  • On the iPhone, it is a sheet from More ▸ Calculator, or from Settings when you keep one section. It has a pad of keys.

How do I use it?

Type a sum: figures, +, - or −, * × or x, / or ÷, brackets and %. Return works it out and leaves the answer to go on from. Esc clears it, and Esc again closes it. In Mortgage, Car Loan and Converters, Esc closes the calculator. ↑ and ↓ bring back the sums before. Copy Answer (⇧⌘C on the Mac) copies the answer.

How it works things out

  • It is decimal, not binary, so 0.1 + 0.2 is 0.3.
  • × and ÷ come before + and −, from the left, with brackets first.
  • A % is per hundred, and after + or − it is a share of what comes before: 80 + 15% is 92.
  • It uses the device’s decimal mark and its thousands mark between figures.

When a sum can’t be worked out it says why: “That isn’t a sum”, “Nothing can be divided by nought.” or “The answer is too large to show.”

What it keeps

What it worked out is kept in memory only, fifty sums at most, and forgotten when the app locks. Copy Answer touches the clipboard only when you choose it, and marks what it copies for this device only. The mortgage and car loan calculators can keep a scenario in your budget, if you choose.

With Loans and Converters on, the calculator also has mortgage and car loan calculators and converters. See The mortgage calculator.

The mortgage calculator

Work out a payment and what extra payments save, with a rate you type.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Calculator, turn on Loans and Converters. It is off to start. The calculator then has a choice at its top: Sums, Mortgage, Car Loan and Converters, and on the Mac they are in the View menu too. With it off, the calculator is exactly as it was.

Every rate is one you type: nothing is looked up, and no rates come with the app. What you type is forgotten when the app locks, unless you save it as a scenario.

What you enter

  • The price, and the down payment as an amount or a percent.
  • The interest rate in percent a year, and the term in years. 15 and 30 years are shown side by side.
  • Property tax a year, home insurance a year, PMI in percent a year, and HOA dues a month, if you have them.
  • Extra payments: a monthly amount, a yearly amount, which is paid with every twelfth payment, and one amount once, at a payment number.

Typed fields are read as sums, so “250,000” and “250000 × 0.2” both work, with or without a currency sign, and a rate’s “6.5%” is 6.5 percent. Each extra goes to what you owe.

What it shows

  • The monthly payment, broken down into principal and interest, tax, insurance, PMI and HOA.
  • The payoff month, the interest in all, and what the extra payments save in interest and in months.
  • PMI is paid until you owe 80% of the price, and not at all with 20% down. It shows how long PMI is paid and what it comes to.

The schedule

The schedule shows by year, with each year’s months under it, or by payment. Export Schedule saves it as a spreadsheet or a CSV file, after saying the file isn’t encrypted. On the Mac it is saved where you choose, and on the iPhone it is handed to the iPhone’s own sheet for sending or saving.

Saving a scenario

While your own budget is open, Mortgage has a part called Saved in Your Budget. Save Scenario… asks for a name, and keeps the figures you typed, as you typed them, in your budget’s encrypted file. An empty name becomes “Mortgage 1”. Choose a saved scenario to bring its figures back, and use the trash button beside it to delete it. It is not offered in the sample budget. A scenario saved by a later version of the app can’t be read by this one, and it says so.

The car loan calculator

Compare 36, 48, 60 and 72 months for the car you are thinking of.

Turn on Loans and Converters in Settings ▸ Features: Calculator, then choose Car Loan at the top of the calculator. Every rate is one you type: nothing is looked up, and what you type is forgotten when the app locks, unless you save it as a scenario.

What you enter

  • The car: the price, the down payment, the trade-in’s value and what is owed on it.
  • Sales tax in percent, and fees.
  • The APR, in percent a year.

Sales tax is on the price less the trade-in, and never below nothing. Fees, and anything owed beyond the trade-in’s value, are borrowed with the car.

What it shows

Terms Compared shows 36, 48, 60 and 72 months side by side: the payment, the interest, the total of payments, and the total cost, which is the price, the tax, the fees and the interest.

Saving a scenario

While your own budget is open, Car Loan has a part called Saved in Your Budget. Save Scenario… asks for a name, and keeps the figures you typed, as you typed them, in your budget’s encrypted file. An empty name becomes “Car Loan 1”. Choose a saved scenario to bring its figures back, and use the trash button beside it to delete it. It is not offered in the sample budget. A scenario saved by a later version of the app can’t be read by this one, and it says so.

Converters

Units, dates, tips and amounts at a rate you type.

How do I turn them on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Calculator, turn on Loans and Converters, then choose Converters at the top of the calculator. Converters has a second choice: Units, Dates, Tip or Currency. Nothing is fetched, and what you type is forgotten when the app locks.

Units

Choose a measure: Length, Weight, Volume, Temperature, Area, Speed or Cooking. Type a value, choose From and To, and the answer shows. Swap turns them round. Units are named as the system names them.

Dates

  • Days Between counts the days between two dates.
  • A Date and Some Days gives the date a number of days away, with the weekday. Type a minus for days before.

Tip

Type the bill, the tip percent and the number of people. It shows the tip, the total, and what each pays, rounded up to the cent. When the shares come to a little more than the total, it says by how much.

Currency

Type an amount and a rate, and the answer shows both ways. The rate is the one you type: no rates come with the app, and none are looked up.

The Clock beside your work

On the Mac: timers, the stopwatch, the world clock and the calculator as panels.

View ▸ Panels offers Timers, Stopwatch and World Clock while the Clock is kept, and Calculator whatever you keep. Each is a panel round the section on screen, which docks, stacks and collapses as the other panels do. See Panels, and your own layout.

A timer started or a lap marked in a panel is the Clock’s own. New Timer… and Add City… at a panel’s foot open the Clock on that sheet.

Deleting in the Clock

A deleted alarm, timer, city or countdown waits in the Clock’s Recently Deleted.

A deleted alarm, timer, city or countdown goes to the Clock’s Recently Deleted. Choose it there to put it back. An alarm put back is off until you turn it on.

Delete Now… and Empty Recently Deleted… clear it for good. Nothing else is touched.

Backups and export

The Clock’s alarms, timers, cities and countdowns aren’t in Export Everything, and the sheet says so. They are kept with the calendar and reminders, so Save a Copy… of the calendar and reminders carries them.
