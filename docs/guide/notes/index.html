Writing a note

Start typing. There is nothing to save.

  • On the Mac, press ⌘N and type.
  • On the iPhone, tap the pencil and type.

There is nothing to save: your words are saved as you type.

The title and the list

A note’s title is its first line. The list shows pinned notes first, then the one you changed most recently.

Plain text

A note is plain text. The app keeps its words exactly as they are in the note.

Finding a note

Search looks through every word of every note as you type.

A note is found when each word you type starts a word in it, in any order: “gro lis” finds “Grocery list”.

Capitals and accents don’t matter. Notes whose title matches come first. Recently Deleted is not searched.

Folders, checklists and formatting

The switches that add more to a note.

Settings ▸ Features: Notes turns on more: Folders, Checklists and Simple formatting. Each is off until you turn it on. On the Mac, Features at the foot of the sidebar shows the same switches. The kinds of note, Tags, Templates and Saved Views have their own switches in the same place.

Turning a feature off hides it and changes nothing you wrote. Turn it on again and everything is as it was.

How formatting is written

Checklists and formatting are plain marks in your own text.

Checklists and formatting are plain Markdown marks in your note’s own text, so an export stays readable. The marks stay visible, faint, so what you see is exactly what’s saved.

  • # Heading is a heading. ## and ### make smaller ones.
  • **bold** is bold, and *italics* is italics.
  • [words](https://…) is a link.
  • - item is a bulleted list, and 1. item a numbered one.
  • - [ ] item is a checklist item, and - [x] item once it is ticked.
  • Three backticks on a line of their own begin a block of code, with a language’s name after them, and three more end it. See Code notes.

Links open only when you ask. There are no previews and no pictures from the web.

Your notes as files

Export your notes as plain text files, or bring a folder of them in.

Export Notes…

In Settings ▸ Export: Notes, Export Notes… writes one plain text file for each note into a new folder inside the one you choose: .md with Checklists or Simple formatting on, .txt otherwise. Each of your folders becomes a folder there. Recently Deleted is left out.

The files aren’t encrypted, so anyone who can open that folder can read them. The app asks before it writes them.

Bring In Notes…

In the same place, Bring In Notes… reads a folder of .md or .txt files. Each file becomes a note, nothing in the folder is changed, and words already here aren’t brought in twice.

Tags and templates

Group notes by a word, and start new ones from a template.

Tags (Mac)

Turn on Tags in Settings ▸ Features: Notes, then write a word with # in front of it anywhere in a note, such as #garden. The sidebar lists every tag with how many notes hold it. Choose one to see those notes. A # followed by a space starts a heading, not a tag, and a tag needs at least one letter.

Capitals and accents don’t matter: #Garden and #garden are one tag. Nothing is added to a note for its tags. They are read from its words.

Templates

Turn on Templates in Settings ▸ Features: Notes, and any note that holds #template is a template. The template stays as it is.

  • On the Mac, Notes ▸ New Note from Template… (⌥⌘N) lists them, and so does the New Note button beside search. Choose one to start a new note with its words, without the #template.
  • On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil to see them, and choose one to start a new note with its words, without the #template.

Saved views in your notes

On the Mac: keep a search, a tag or a folder as a named view.

How do I save one?

  1. In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Saved Views.
  2. Show a search, a tag or a folder in the list.
  3. Choose Notes ▸ Save This View… (⌥⌘S) and give it a name.

Using your views

Your views are in the sidebar, under Saved Views. Choose one to show its tag or its folder, and its search, again. Control-click a view to rename it, move it up or down, or delete it, or drag it to another place.

A deleted view waits in Recently Deleted, like a note. Export Notes… writes your views beside your notes, in a file named Saved Views.csv.

Kinds of note

Pick Journal, Recipe, Logins, Binder, Code or Authenticator when you make a note.

A kind of note is still a note. It is stored in the same encrypted place and opens with the same unlock. You choose the kind when you make the note.

How do I turn a kind on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Authenticator, Journal, Recipes, Logins, Binder or Code. Each is off to start. The password generator has a switch there too.

How do I make one?

  • New Note stays quick. On the Mac, ⌘N and, on the iPhone, a tap on the pencil still make a plain note at once.
  • On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil to see the list.
  • On the Mac, use the New Note button beside the search field. Its arrow opens the list, which shows once there is more than a plain note to offer.

The list holds Plain Note, then each kind whose switch is on, then From Your Templates while Templates is on.

The kinds

  • Journal, one entry for each day.
  • Recipes, to cook from.
  • Logins, usernames and passwords.
  • Binder, what your family would need if something happened to you.
  • Code, code in a monospace face, coloured by its language.
  • Authenticator, sign-in codes for two-step sign-in.

A kind is chosen when a note is made and never changed. Search, the recovery sheet and export treat each kind as the page for it says.

Journal

One entry for each day, with a mood if you like.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Journal. It is off to start, on both devices.

Writing today’s entry

  • Choose Journal in New Note’s list, or use the command box. On the Mac, Notes ▸ Today’s Journal Entry (⌃⌘J) opens today’s entry, or starts it.
  • An entry is typed as a plain note is, and saves as you type.

One entry for each day

The day and an optional mood belong to the entry, not to its words, so typing can never move an entry to another day. An entry’s day never changes once it is stored.

  • The entry’s page has a strip of the last fourteen days. Each day you have written is marked.
  • Choose any day on the strip to open that day’s entry, or to start it. A day after today can’t be chosen.
  • A menu holds five moods: Hard, Low, Okay, Good and Great. Or choose none.

The list names each entry by its day, with its first words under it.

Export

An exported entry has a first line such as journal: 2026-11-21, with , mood: good if it has one, before its words. Bring In Notes… brings it back as an entry of that day.

Recipes

Recipes you can cook from, with amounts that scale.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Recipes. It is off to start, on both devices.

  • On the Mac, choose Notes ▸ New Recipe.
  • On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil and choose Recipe.

What a recipe holds

A recipe has a name, its servings, prep and cook times in minutes, tags, ingredients and numbered steps. It is stored as plain text: the name on the first line, then lines that each start with a word of the format: servings:, prep:, cook:, tag:, ingredient: and step:. Other lines are kept after them.

Cooking from it

The screen shows the recipe to cook from: its times, its tags, Serves with − and + that scales every ingredient’s amount, the ingredients, and the numbered steps. The scaling is for this look only. It isn’t kept.

Change Recipe… opens a form: name, servings, prep and cook minutes, tags, and the ingredients and the steps, one on each line. It is one change, and Undo takes it back.

How scaling works

An amount at the start of an ingredient, such as 2, 1.5, 1/2, 1 1/2 or ½, is multiplied by the new servings over the old. It is written back as a whole number, a common fraction (halves, thirds, quarters or eighths) or one decimal place.

Finding a recipe

Search reads the name, the ingredients, the steps and the tags. A recipe’s tags are its own, listed with it. They are not the notes’ # tags.

Logins

Keep usernames and passwords in a note, with no autofill and no connection to the internet.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Logins. It is off to start, on both devices.

  • On the Mac, choose Notes ▸ New Logins Note, or Logins from the New Note button beside search.
  • On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil and choose Logins.

What a login holds

Each login has a site, a username, a password, an address and notes. A Logins note shows the note’s name, then each login’s site, username and address, with the password as dots. The note takes no typing: each change is one of the notes’ own actions, with one Undo step.

Adding a login

  1. Choose Add Login….
  2. Fill in Site, User Name, Password, Address and Notes. Generate Password…, beside Password, opens the password generator in place and puts its password in the field.
  3. Save it. The first login waits for the notes’ recovery sheet, as the first Authenticator account does.

Change Login… and Delete Login… are on each login. Delete asks first. On the Mac, you can drag the logins into another order.

Seeing and copying a password

  • Show Password shows one password for 30 seconds, then hides it again.
  • Copy Password and Copy User Name copy for this device only, and clear the clipboard after 90 seconds if it is still there.
  • The first time you Show or Copy Password after the app unlocks, it asks you to show the device is yours: Face ID or Touch ID, or the passcode or the Mac’s password. Once passed, passwords and sign-in codes both show until the app locks. A Logins note doesn’t ask when it opens, so you may open it for a username.

What it is not

There is no autofill, no browser extension and no network. By default, anyone who can unlock your device can open these passwords. For a lock of their own, turn on a passphrase for the notes in Settings ▸ The lock. The note says so under the logins.

Search and export

  • Search holds the note’s name and each login’s site, username and address, never a password or a login’s notes.
  • Export Notes… and Export Everything leave the passwords and each login’s own notes out unless you choose to put them in, which asks first that this is your device. Export Notes… offers Choose a Folder and Include Passwords…. Export Everything has a switch, off to start.
  • Export as CSV…, on a Logins note, writes its logins in the columns Title, URL, Username, Password and Notes, so you can move them to another app. It warns you first that the file holds every password as plain text.
  • Bring In Notes… brings an exported Logins note back as one.

The password generator

Make a password or a PIN on your device, with no internet.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Password Generator. It is off to start, on both devices. Inside Add Login and Change Login it is always there, with Logins.

Where is it?

  • On the Mac, View ▸ Generate Password… (⌃⌘G) opens it in a small window of its own, and the command box finds it there.
  • On the iPhone, tap Generate Password under More, beside the calculator, or in Settings with one section kept.

Three kinds

  • Password is 8 to 64 characters long, from letters, figures and symbols as you choose. With Leave out look-alikes on, which it is to start, characters easily taken for one another (0, O, o, 1, l, I and |) are left out. At least one of each kind you chose is always in it.
  • PIN is 4 to 12 figures. It says it is meant for a lock that stops after a few wrong tries.
  • Groups of Six are three groups of six letters and figures joined by dashes, such as xxxxxx-xxxxxx-xxxxxx, with one capital and one figure among them.

How strong it is

It shows the strength in bits, worked out exactly from the number of passwords the settings can make, never from the password shown: “About 89 bits: very strong”. The word is weak under 40 bits, fair under 60, strong under 80 and very strong from 80.

Copying

Copy copies for this device only, and clears the clipboard after 90 seconds if it is still there.

A binder for your family

A note for what your family would need to know, with a PDF to print.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Binder. On the Mac, choose Notes ▸ New Binder. On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil and choose Binder. A new binder is named Important Information, until you change it.

What a binder holds

Its sections are ready: Emergency contacts, Doctors and medications, Insurance, Bank and retirement accounts, Where the papers are, Who to call, Online accounts and Anything else. Choose Add… in a section to fill it in, and leave the rest empty.

  • Each entry has a Title, Details and, if you want one, a Number.
  • Change… and Delete… are on each entry. Delete asks first, and Undo brings it back.
  • In the list of notes, a binder says how many entries it has, not what is in them.

Numbers

An account, policy or member number shows as dots until you show this is your device, the same as a password. Show Number, Hide Number and Copy Number are on the entry.

Online accounts

Online accounts are named by site only. Add Sites from Your Logins adds the sites in your Logins notes by name. No password ever goes in the binder.

Making a PDF

Make a PDF… makes the binder’s pages on your device, to print or hand to someone you trust. The PDF isn’t encrypted, and it says so before it is made. Numbers show only their last four characters unless you choose Include Numbers…, which asks first that this is your device.

The lock

By default, anyone who can unlock your device can open the binder. For a lock of its own, turn on Lock the notes with a passphrase in Settings ▸ The lock.

Code notes

Keep code in a note, coloured by its language, and never run.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Code. On the Mac, choose Notes ▸ New Code Note. On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil and choose Code. A new one is named Untitled Code, until you change it.

What a Code note is

  • Its words are in a monospace face, coloured by the language you choose from the menu above it: Swift, Python, JavaScript and TypeScript, HTML, CSS, JSON, YAML, Markdown, Shell, SQL or Plain Text. On the Mac, they have line numbers too.
  • Nothing in a Code note is ever run. It keeps the code, and that is all.
  • On the Mac, Tab puts in spaces, Shift-Tab takes them out, and Return keeps the line’s indent. On the iPhone, Return keeps the line’s indent.

Finding and copying

On the Mac, Notes ▸ Find and Replace… (⌥⌘F) finds words in a Code note and changes them. On the iPhone, the magnifying glass does. Copy All copies the whole note.

Export, and code inside other notes

Export Notes… writes a Code note as a file of its language, such as .py or .swift, and Bring In Notes… brings such a file back as a Code note.

With Simple formatting on, three backticks on a line of their own begin a block of code in any note, with a language’s name after them, and three more end it. See How formatting is written.

Sign-in codes (Authenticator)

Keep the codes for two-step sign-in in a note, and see each one as it changes.

How do I set it up?

  1. In Settings ▸ Features: Notes, turn on Authenticator.
  2. On the Mac, choose Notes ▸ New Authenticator Note (⌃⌘N). On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil and choose Authenticator.
  3. Choose Add Account…, and type or paste the key a site shows when you turn on two-step sign-in with an authenticator app.

Add Account… also has More Options: Algorithm (SHA-1, SHA-256 or SHA-512), Digits (6 or 8) and New code every (30, 60 or 15 seconds). Change these only if the site says to. Most use SHA-1, six digits and 30 seconds.

Using the codes

The note shows each account’s code as it changes, with a ring for the seconds it has left. Click or tap a code to copy it.

  • On the Mac, the app takes the code off the clipboard after a minute, or as it quits, unless you’ve copied something since. Universal Clipboard doesn’t offer it on your other devices.
  • On the iPhone, it stays on this iPhone’s clipboard for a minute, and Universal Clipboard doesn’t offer it on your other devices.

The check before codes show

The first time you open an Authenticator note after unlocking the app, it asks for Touch ID or your Mac’s password (on the iPhone, Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode) before it shows the codes. If you cancel, the names still show and the codes stay hidden, with Show Codes to ask again.

Looking after your accounts

  • Change Names… changes the site’s and your own names for an account, never its key or how its codes are made. A new key is a new account.
  • Delete Account… asks first. Before you delete one, turn off two-step sign-in at the site, or be sure you can sign in another way. Undo takes it back.
  • Your accounts are kept in the note, with your notes, encrypted. The app asks you to make a recovery sheet before the first account, and it’s wise to keep each site’s own backup codes too.
  • Search finds an Authenticator note by its name and its accounts’ names, never by a key. Export Notes… writes each account’s key as readable text, and says so before it writes anything.
  • If you turn Authenticator off, it stops being offered when you make a note, and notes already made keep opening and showing their codes.

The keyboard in your notes

Spelling, Writing Tools and what the keyboard learns.

  • On the Mac, spelling is corrected and checked as you type in your notes. To change that, Control-click in a note and choose Spelling and Grammar.
  • On the iPhone, spelling is corrected and checked as you type. Settings ▸ Notes: the keyboard changes that.

Apple’s Writing Tools and inline predictions are off in your notes and their search.

Apple doesn’t say that any app’s settings stop the keyboard learning, so this app doesn’t claim to.

VoiceOver in your notes

Every button and field has a name VoiceOver reads.

  • With Simple formatting on, VoiceOver’s rotor lists a note’s headings and links and moves from one to the next. On the Mac, press Control-Option-U. On the iPhone, turn two fingers on the screen.
  • The marks themselves (#, **, - [ ]) are read as written. How much of them you hear follows VoiceOver’s punctuation setting. On the iPhone, VoiceOver says “heading” as it reads one.
  • With Checklists on, Tick or Untick is a VoiceOver action on the note. On the iPhone, swipe up or down to it.
  • In an Authenticator note, VoiceOver hears an account’s names, then its code a digit at a time and the seconds left, with Copy Code as the hint.

Deleted notes

Deleted notes wait in Recently Deleted until you empty it.

A deleted note waits in Recently Deleted until you empty it. Nothing is removed after a number of days. Choose it there to put it back, and you can change it again.

When you empty Recently Deleted, the words are wiped from your notes on this device. Backups made before then, Time Machine’s and iCloud’s among them, keep them until they age out.
