Adding a purchase

Type an amount and a payee, check the category, and add it.

How do I add one?

  • On the Mac, with the Budget showing, press ⌘N. Type the amount, then the payee: choose one you’ve used before from the list under the field, and it fills in the category it had last time. Check the category, then click Add.
  • On the iPhone, tap Add at the bottom of a budget screen. Type the amount, tap Next, then type the payee: tap one you’ve used before in the list under it, and it fills in the category it had last time. Check the category, then tap Add.

Money in, transfers and more

Money out is the usual kind. Choose money in or a transfer at the top. Yesterday is one tap on the sheet itself. The account, another date and a memo are under More.

Once it’s saved, the sheet says what that category has left, with Undo beside it.

Bills and paydays ask first

Nothing is recorded by itself. A bill waits for your one tap.

Nothing is recorded by itself. On its due day a bill shows under Waiting for You on Home, with Paid, Different Amount… and Not Yet. A payday asks the same way, with It Came.

One tap records the transaction with the bill’s amount, account and category. Typing the payment yourself counts just the same.

On the calendar and in Reminders

With the Calendar or the Reminders kept too, the “Bills on the Calendar and in Reminders” switch, in Settings ▸ Features: Bills, shows a bill on its due day in the calendar, and with today’s reminders while it waits. Among the reminders, Paid, Different Amount… and Not Yet work as they do on Home.

Where you stand, and safe to spend

Home’s gauge says whether the month is in good shape, tight or short, and shows its working.

The gauge

Home’s gauge says whether the month is in good shape, tight or short: one track, red at the left for short, yellow in the middle for tight and green at the right for good shape, with a pointer above it where the month stands. The sentence under it says the same in words. It comes from three answers: what’s safe to spend, whether this month’s bills are covered, and your pace through the month.

The further left the pointer, the worse the month. Each third of the track is one of the three words, and inside its third the pointer follows the same answers. When short, it’s further left the further below zero the money would go, against what you have now and the pay still to come this month. When tight, it’s further left when nothing is safe to spend, when bills would take more of the money set aside, or the further behind your pace is. In good shape, the plan’s day itself is the middle of its third, and spending slower moves it right.

Hiding the gauge

  • On the Mac, turn off Show the Gauge in Settings, under Home, or Control-click Where You Stand and choose Hide Gauge. It’s hidden on this Mac only, and the sentence stays.
  • On the iPhone, turn off Show the Gauge in Settings, under Home. It’s hidden on this iPhone only, and the sentence stays.

Safe to spend

Safe to spend is what your everyday categories have left this month, lowered when the plan asks for more money than there is. It never goes below zero.

Can I Afford It?

Can I Afford It? answers for one purchase. When the answer is yes but the purchase would still make the month tight or short, a second line says so.

How it was worked out

Under each answer, click (or on the iPhone, tap) How This Was Worked Out. It’s arithmetic on what you typed, nothing more.

Checking a balance with your bank

The app never connects to your bank, so you compare the two yourself.

  1. Open an account and choose Check Balance…. It shows what your budget says the account holds.
  2. If your bank shows the same, choose It Matches, and the account is marked as checked today.
  3. If not, choose It Doesn’t and type the bank’s figure. You’ll see the difference, and can mark recent transactions as not at the bank yet.

Still off? Add what was missed, fix a typo, or add a correction: one entry, clearly labelled.

The plan, and changing it

What each category gets, this month and the months ahead.

On the Mac

Plan is a grid: each category down the side, with what’s spent and what’s left this month, then what’s planned for each of the next twelve months, and the twelve added up. Each group’s line adds up its categories, and the line along the foot adds up them all.

Type a figure in a month to change it: it becomes that category’s amount for that month of the year, every year, as Set by Month… sets it, and this month’s also changes this month’s plan. A figure the plan would give anyway is left to the plan. With Envelope budgeting on, amounts are set on the Budget screen instead, and the months are read only. Right-click a category or a group to rename, hide or move it.

On the iPhone

Plan lists each category with its amount this month, what’s spent and what’s left. Change an amount for this month only or from now on, or set different amounts for different months of the year. With Envelope budgeting on, amounts are set on the Budget screen instead. Categories and groups can be added, renamed, hidden and moved.

A budget is meant to change from month to month. Changing it isn’t falling behind.

Working in the grid

On the Mac: an account’s register and the Plan are grids you edit in place.

An account’s register and Plan are grids. Click a cell, or move with the arrow keys. Tab goes along a row and on to the next. Type to replace what’s in the chosen cell, or press Return or click it again to change it as it is. While typing, Return keeps it and goes down, Tab keeps it and goes right, and Esc leaves the cell as it was.

Undo, several rows and copying down

Each change is one step of Edit ▸ Undo. Shift with the up or down arrow, or a click with Shift, chooses more rows, and the totals row along the foot then adds up just those. ⌘D copies the first chosen row’s cell down to the others.

In a register

Click a column’s heading to sort by it, again for the other way, and a third time for the register’s own order. A split’s lines, the other account of a transfer, and a row’s account are changed in the entry strip above the grid: typing in one of those cells opens it. In the cleared column, Space marks the chosen rows cleared or not. New transactions are added with ⌘N, as before.

Copying and pasting rows

On the Mac: copy rows to a spreadsheet, or paste rows in, always shown first.

Copying

In the grid, ⌘C copies the chosen rows the way a spreadsheet app takes them. They’re marked for this Mac only, so Universal Clipboard doesn’t offer them on your other devices. While they’re on the clipboard, any app on this Mac can read them, as with anything you copy.

Pasting into a register

⌘V in a register reads rows copied from a spreadsheet as new transactions, in the register’s own order of columns from Date to Inflow, or in any order when the first row names the columns. A column named Amount is one figure, below zero for money out. In All Accounts an Account column comes after Date.

Before anything is added, a sheet shows each row as it was read, and why any row can’t be kept. Choose Keep, and each row is added as a change of its own. Edit ▸ Undo takes the whole paste back. A paste never changes a transaction already there.

Pasting into the plan

⌘V in Plan puts figures in the months from the chosen cell, rightward and downward, shown first in the same way. Only the months change.

More for the budget

The switches that add envelopes, income planning, reports and more.

Settings ▸ Features: Budget turns on more: Envelope budgeting (the Budget screen and Ready to Assign), Income planning, Reports, and Advanced transactions (Reconcile and a budget year). Net worth, Debt payoff and Retirement planning are switches there too. Each is off until you turn it on. On the Mac, Features at the foot of the sidebar shows the same switches.

Turning a feature off hides its screens again. What you entered stays.

Bills on the calendar and in Reminders has its own switch, in Settings ▸ Features: Bills. See Bills and paydays ask first.

Net worth

What you own, what you owe, and the difference, with a chart over one, two or three years.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Budget, turn on Net worth. It is off to start. Net Worth then gets a place of its own: in the Mac’s sidebar, and under More on the iPhone.

What it shows

What is owned and what is owed today, each account with its balance, the totals, and a chart over one, two or three years. Accounts outside the budget are marked so. It is typed by hand, so Stonefold never connects to a bank.

Keeping values up to date

  • Update Values… lists each account outside the budget, with what it holds now and a field for its value now (for a loan, what is owed). Save writes one entry for each one changed, labelled “Value update” and dated today, in one Undo step.
  • Add an Asset… opens the new account sheet with Type already set to “Asset or Investment (tracking only)”, and Include in budget off.

A value is a correction, as a balance check’s is, so the history and the chart follow it.

Debt payoff

Compare ways to pay off what you owe, and see how long each takes.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Budget, turn on Debt payoff. It is off to start, and Debt Payoff then gets a place of its own.

What you enter

Each card and loan account that you owe on is listed with its balance, and has fields for its rate (APR) and its minimum. You can also type in debts by name and balance. Then set the extra you can pay each month, and the order.

The orders

  • Smallest balance first.
  • Highest rate first.
  • Your own order.

Each month interest is added and every minimum is paid. What is left of the minimums and the extra goes to the first debt in the order still owed, then the next. A debt paid off frees its minimum for the rest. The order is fixed from the starting balances and rates.

What it shows

When each debt is paid off, the interest in all, the smallest-balance and highest-rate orders side by side, and the plan month by month, starting next month. If a plan would take more than 100 years, it says “Not in 100 years”. What you type in Debt Payoff is forgotten when the app locks, unless you choose Save Plan, which keeps it in your budget. When Debt Payoff opens with nothing typed, the saved plan comes back.

Saving the plan

Save Plan keeps one plan in your own budget’s encrypted file: each account’s APR and minimum, the debts you typed in, the extra and the order. It is not offered in the sample budget. A line says where the plan stands: “Not saved”, “Saved in your budget” or “Changed since it was saved”. Go Back to the Saved Plan brings the saved one back, and Delete the Saved Plan… takes it out of your budget; what is on screen stays until the app locks.

The retirement planner

See how a plan holds up, year by year, with your own numbers.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Budget, turn on Retirement planning. It is off to start. The Mac’s budget sidebar then gains Retirement, after Reports, and the command box finds it and New Retirement Plan…. Each one is a “retirement plan”: “Plan” is already the budget’s monthly plan. Plans are kept in the budget’s file, encrypted. On the iPhone, Retirement is in the budget’s list of screens.

Starting a plan

New Retirement Plan… asks four things: the year you were born, the age you’d like to retire at, what you spend a year, and what you earn a year. It fills in the rest. It counts your budget’s own accounts, other than cards and loans, as cash, and an account you track outside the budget only if you choose it, as taxable: count investments, not a house or a car. You set each one’s type. To start it assumes an end age of 95, inflation 3%, tax 15%, returns of 6% and 2%, volatility 12% and a safe withdrawal rate of 4%. The plan’s page says what it assumed, each with Change beside it. On the Mac, click any part of the plan to change it, or + beside a list to add to it. On the iPhone, tap New Retirement Plan… to make one, tap any part of it to change it, or + beside a list to add to it.

The page

The answer comes first: whether your money lasts to 95 or runs out at an age, the chance of success, and the target and when it is met. Then a chart of net worth over your life, with retirement and milestones marked, and the plan’s parts in short lists: Accounts, Income, Spending, Saving, Milestones and Social Security. Each part is a sheet. On the Mac, By Year shows every year: age, income, Social Security, taxes, spending, saved, taken out and net worth, in today’s money or that year’s. On the iPhone, By Year shows every year’s net worth in today’s money. Change… opens More About This Plan: the ages, the rates, the withdrawal order, the leftover account, the safe rate and the number of tries.

Comparing plans

To compare, choose Duplicate from the plan’s menu, then change the copy: retiring at 60 and at 65, say. On the Mac, Compare draws up to three plans side by side.

What goes in a plan

  • Accounts: cash, taxable, tax-deferred (such as a 401(k) or traditional IRA), Roth and HSA. Each balance is read from one of your budget’s accounts, or typed, and grows at the plan’s rate for its type, or a rate of its own.
  • Streams: income, spending, and contributions into an account, by you or by an employer. Each is in today’s money and rises with prices unless you say not, from now, an age, retirement or a milestone, up to another.
  • Milestones: retiring, and any others at an age: a house, college, a windfall. Each can bring money once, and money yearly for some years.
  • Social Security: typed from your own statement: the monthly benefit at full retirement age (67 for anyone born in 1960 or later, or set yours) and the age you will claim it, 62 to 70. The claiming age changes the benefit by the Social Security Act’s rule for early and late claims. It rises with prices.
  • Taxes: one rate you type, on taxed income and Social Security less what is put in before tax, on all of a tax-deferred withdrawal, and on the growth share of a taxable one.
  • Withdrawals: when a year spends more than comes in, from the accounts in a plain order: cash, taxable, tax-deferred, Roth, HSA. You can change the order under More.

The target and the chance of success

The target is a year of retirement’s spending over a safe withdrawal rate, in today’s money, and the age at which your accounts first hold it. The chance of success is worked out from a thousand lives on your device, each drawing every year’s market return from a normal curve with the expected return as its middle and a volatility you set as its spread. Cash never moves with the markets. The same plan always gives the same chance. It is shown beside the plain projection, with the band one life in ten either side.

What it leaves out

It is an estimate, not financial advice. Today is taken as the start of this year. A year grows first, and its money comes and goes at its end. Growth isn’t taxed until it is taken out. No tax tables, early-withdrawal penalties, contribution limits or required withdrawals are counted, and the plan follows one person’s ages: a partner’s income is a stream from an age. Like everything Stonefold works out, it is plain arithmetic, with no AI.

The sample budget

Look around a made-up household before you set up your own.

Look Around a Sample Budget, offered in the Budget section before you choose Set Up Your Budget, opens a made-up household with three years of spending, bills and pay. Try anything in it.

Nothing in it is kept: it goes when you leave it, and it’s made fresh next time. Start My Own Budget takes you to a budget of your own.

Budgeting words

Every word the budget uses, in plain English, with an example.

Each word has one short meaning and an everyday example. Words that belong to a feature only show when that feature is on.

Activity
What went in or out of a category this month; spending shows as a minus. For example: Two grocery trips of 80 and 45 make Groceries’ activity −125. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Assigned
What you give a category this month, to spend or to keep. For example: Assigning 600 to Groceries in October plans 600 of food for October. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Available
What a category has left now: what it carried in, plus what was assigned, plus its activity. For example: 600 assigned and 125 spent leave 475 available. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Budget Accounts
Accounts you budget from, such as checking, savings and cards. For example: Checking, savings and your cards: the money your budget and Safe to Spend count.
Carried In
What a category had left at the end of last month, which it keeps this month. For example: 40 left in Dining Out on September 30 starts October with 40 carried in. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Cleared
Transactions the bank has already posted. For example: A purchase on your October statement is cleared.
Due
A bill not paid yet this month, with its due day still to come. For example: A streaming service is due October 28. Until you enter its payment, it reads Due.
Features
Extra tools you can turn on in Settings, such as envelope budgeting, income planning and reports. For example: Settings ▸ Features: turn on Reports, and Reports appears in the sidebar.
Funded
A bill or target already has all the money it needs this month. For example: Internet costs 89.99 and its category holds 89.99: funded before it’s due. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Overdue
Its due day passed and no payment has been entered. For example: Water was due October 5, and by October 23 no payment has been entered.
Overspent
A category spent more than it had, so its Available is below zero. For example: Dining Out had 50 and spent 70: it’s 20 overspent until you cover it from another category. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Paid
This month’s payment for the bill is in your transactions. For example: Electric’s 188.60 payment on October 15 marks October paid.
Partial
Some of what a bill needs this month is set aside, but not all of it. For example: 50 set aside for an 89.99 bill is partial: it needs 39.99 more. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Projected End of Month
The money with no job left at the month’s end, once pay still expected arrives and the budget is spent. For example: 20,000 in cash, 500 of bills to pay, 1,000 still budgeted and 2,000 of pay to come: 20,500. Shown with Income planning on.
Ready to Assign
Money you have that isn’t planned for anything yet: give it a job by assigning it to categories. For example: You were paid 3,000 and gave 2,500 to categories: 500 is Ready to Assign. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Reconcile
Checking your budget’s balance against a statement’s, then locking the transactions you checked. For example: The statement says 1,000 on October 9 and so does your budget: one click marks those rows checked. Shown with Advanced transactions on.
Safe to Spend
What’s left this month for everyday things and fun, after bills and money set aside for later. For example: Everyday has 420 left and Just for Fun 120: 540 is safe to spend, about 108 a day for 5 days.
Starting Balance
What an account held on its first day in your budget. For example: A card that owed 412 on July 1 starts at −412.
Target
What a category aims to have, every month or by a date, so you know how much to assign. For example: A 600-a-month Groceries target asks for 600 each month; Assign to All Targets gives it. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Tracking Accounts
Accounts you watch without budgeting from them, such as a mortgage or investments. For example: A 401(k) shows its balance and history, but isn’t divided into categories.
Transfer
Money moved between two of your accounts: not income, and not spending. For example: Paying the card from checking is a transfer: both balances change, and no category does.
True Expenses
Costs that come now and then, saved for a little each month so they don’t catch you out. For example: Car repairs, gifts, a yearly subscription: 36 a month for the car is there at its next service. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Uncleared
Transactions you’ve entered that the bank hasn’t posted yet. For example: A payment you entered today, before the bank has posted it.
Unfunded
Nothing is set aside for a bill yet this month. For example: Set Aside on the Dashboard assigns what the bills due soon still need. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Working Balance
Cleared plus uncleared: what the account holds once everything posts. For example: 1,200 cleared + −200 uncleared = 1,000 working balance.
