# CLAUDE.md — rules for the Stonefold Labs website

> **This is the launch version, written 2026-11-21 and live for the owner's review at his word that day** ("Please make the launch day website live so that it can be reviewed. I'm not worried about anyone stumbling across the website right now so it's only me seeing it."). It describes the app as it will be on launch day, sync included, so it is not to be announced, linked or shown to anyone else until the app ships. The old site is tagged `pre-launch-site-2026-11-21`. `LAUNCH-BRANCH.md` has what it holds and what to do on launch day. Where a rule below says "launch branch", it holds here.

This repo is the public website for stonefoldapp.com (the company is Stonefold Labs; the site moved from stonefoldlabs.com on the owner's word, "Make it the main", 2026-11-18, and `PUBLISHING.md` Part 9 is the switch). Read `README.md` first. The Stonefold house rules (`../Stonefold/HOUSE-RULES.md`) hold here: §4 (what may be claimed), §5 (the website line), §7 and §8 (names and brand), §11 (the game). For what the app does, `../StonefoldApp/SPEC.md` is the record: §4 (three files, one lock), §6 (what may be claimed) and §21.2 (the app's own "What Leaves This Device").

## Non-negotiables
- **Plain pages.** Hand-written HTML and one CSS file. No build step, no framework, no generator.
- **One script.** `docs/theme.js` is the theme picker: the light and dark switch the owner asked for on 2026-11-12, widened on 2026-11-18 to the app's eight themes ("a toggle to be able to switch to any theme that is also in the app"), and on 2026-11-21 to nine with Editor, a code editor's look with several colours (the owner, who had the four terminal themes on the site for a few minutes and found them hard to read: "one theme ... what coding or ides look like"). The app's own Editor theme is decided (2026-11-21, its builder, landing on its `main`), and the site uses its values. It keeps one word in the browser and reaches no network. Its contents are pinned in `scripts/check.py`; change it only on purpose, read the diff, re-pin. **Launch branch:** the demo (`docs/demo.js`, Home's "Try the window", allowed on `main` on 2026-11-18) is gone, on the owner's word of 2026-11-21 ("Lets also get rid of the "try the window" section"), so `theme.js` is the site's one script again. **Then the owner asked for a search in the guide** (2026-11-21: "Can we add a 'search' feature to the wiki/guide", then "Yes, build search" to a description of exactly what it would do), so the guide's pages, and only they, carry two more files: `docs/guide/search.js` and its data, `docs/guide/search-index.js`. The script makes a box and a list of links, finds pages by their words in the browser, and (the owner, 2026-11-21: "can we have it track/highlight the left menu as you scroll?", then "yes build it" to a description of exactly what it would do) marks the topic being read in the list down the left as the page scrolls, setting only `aria-current` on that row; it reaches no network and keeps nothing; it is pinned in `scripts/check.py` like `theme.js`, and the check refuses it any call that could reach out, keep a word or run code. The index is rebuilt from the pages (the builder is in the chat's scratchpad, not here) and the check reads the pages again and refuses an index that differs by a word. Never add another script, and never one from another site. If a page seems to need more (an animation, say), don't add it: describe exactly what it would do and put it to the owner. Fonts are system stacks, with one exception: **Stonefold Display**, the heading font drawn from the wordmark's own letters (the owner, 2026-11-21: "Draw our own heading font from the logo"). It is one small file, `docs/fonts/stonefold-display.woff2`, served from this site, pinned by its SHA-256 in `scripts/check.py`, which also allows exactly one `@font-face` and the CSP's `font-src 'self'` for it, and nothing else. `scripts/build-font.py` makes it (it needs the `fonttools` and `brotli` packages, in a throwaway virtual environment); change a letter there, rebuild, look at it, re-pin. The font also holds one ligature: typed as "Stonefold" (or "stonefold" or "STONEFOLD"), the word comes out as the logo, the wordmark with its two stones (the owner, 2026-11-21: "make stonefold match the logo everywhere that it is named"). Every "Stonefold" in the words of a page is set in `<span class="wm">`, which uses the font, so the name looks like the logo and is still text (copied, searched and read aloud as "Stonefold"); `wm` has no letter-spacing, or the ligature would not form. Not in page titles, pictures, keys or attributes, and not in "Stonefold Labs", the company. The guide's search results show it as plain text. No other font is ever added, and none comes from another site.
- **Nothing that could track a reader.** No analytics, no cookies, no forms, no embeds, no fonts, pictures, scripts or files from another site. Every page carries the same content-security rule; don't loosen it.
- **Claims are promises.** A page says only what house rules §4 and the app's `SPEC.md` §6 allow on that day. Read both again before a sentence about privacy, encryption or security is written or changed. In particular:
  - Nothing about sync or sharing until they ship. "Data Not Collected" waits until a label is live. **Launch branch:** sync ships with the app, so it is said as SPEC §6 allows (end-to-end encrypted, only your devices hold the key, through your own iCloud, no servers of ours), and `scripts/check.py` lets that wording through. Sharing is still not said. His words of 2026-11-21 lift that limit for the owner's review of the site; it is still not announced until sync ships.
  - Nothing about a passphrase, or that only the person can open it, said of the app as a whole. By default anyone who can unlock the device can open the app, and the page says so wherever the lock is described. A passphrase is mentioned only as something a person may add to the budget or the notes, and nothing is said of what it withstands.
  - Nothing says the device's hardware guards a key (`SPEC.md` §4, "True only once builds are signed").
  - Nothing suggests we can get back what a person has lost the key to. "Recovery sheet" is the app's own name for the page a person prints, and is the only use of that word.
  - A feature is described only once it is built and in `SPEC.md`. On 2026-11-15 that left out Export Everything and the one Help (A8).
  - Apple's current pages are read before a claim is written, not remembered. No iPhone or Mac is named by model; the lowest systems are iOS 26 and macOS 26.
- **The app is not described as available** until it is, and no page links to a store until the store page exists. **Launch branch:** the pages say what the app does in the present tense, with no "coming" or "later". The one price on the site is the owner's, chosen 2026-11-18 ("$24.99 for both"): $24.99, paid once, for iPhone and Mac together, said as "costs" on this branch; `scripts/check.py` refuses any other figure. On launch day the store link is added, in the hero buttons and nowhere else, and the check is loosened for that wording only (`LAUNCH-BRANCH.md`). The price waits, like the purchase promise's "one purchase covers iPhone and Mac", on the App Store record being set up that way.
- **Names.** The app is **Stonefold**, the bare word, and the company is **Stonefold Labs** (the owner, 2026-11-13). On 2026-11-18, with the move to stonefoldapp.com, he had the wordmark in the header and footer changed to **Stonefold**; the page titles say "Stonefold" too ("Privacy · Stonefold"), the owner's word the same day. "Stonefold Labs" stays in About, the legal line and the Privacy page. The five apps' names (Bellwether Budget and the rest) are retired and appear nowhere. The game inside the app is mentioned only by the owner's line, word for word: "There is a small flock in here somewhere." **Launch version:** that line is gone from Home too, on the owner's word of 2026-11-21 ("You can get rid of there is a flock in here somewhere"), so no page mentions the game; `scripts/check.py` still refuses any other mention.
- **The company's legal name** is **Stonefold Labs LLC**, a Florida limited liability company (Sunbiz document number L26000574352, filed 2026-11-12, approved and reported by the owner on 2026-11-17). Each page's footer carries "© 2026 Stonefold Labs LLC", and the privacy page says Stonefold Labs LLC runs the website. `COMPANY_FORMED` in `scripts/check.py` is `True`, so the check fails any page without the full name. The full logo (it reads "STONEFOLD LABS LLC" in Avenir Next) stays off the site until its words are outlines and the font's licence is confirmed (`PUBLISHING.md` Part 8).
- **The brand is the owner's.** The mark in `docs/brand/` is the one he picked and the app's logo chat exported (`../StonefoldApp/docs/brand/`, commit 7285e11): eight evenly spaced stones in a square fold with an opening at the bottom, and no star. He chose it for the app's icon on 2026-11-18 ("Lets just do the 8 dashes and get rid of the star"), then made it the company's mark: "company mark too" (house rules §8). It replaces the earlier mark, which he supplied on 2026-11-12 and approved moving to the middle on 2026-11-17. Don't redraw it: its shapes are the app's `docs/brand/` files', and the only thing the site changes is the colour of the stones, to each theme's own accent, as the app's icons do (the owner noticed on 2026-11-18 that a mark that stays one colour doesn't follow the theme). The pages draw it inline, with the stones in the current theme's accent, as the app's icon for each theme does (`README.md`, "The brand" lists them); the two files in `docs/brand/` are the supplied artwork and are not referenced by a page. The tab icon is the evergreen mark on a warm ivory tile, which shows on a dark tab bar too; the app's own favicon uses a `<style>` for dark mode, which the site's picture rules don't allow. The PNG and `.ico` icon set (2026-11-18) is allowed too, but only after `scripts/strip-png.py`. Colours come from the owner's palette (`README.md`, "The brand") and, for the themes, the app's (`README.md`, "The theme picker"). The full logo says "LLC" and sets words in a font, so it stays off the site until its words are outlines. A picture is one `.svg` of this site, with `alt`, `width` and `height`; the one exception is pictures of the app (below). **The wordmark's two o's are the mark's stones** (the owner, 2026-11-21); the rest of the name is as drawn on 2026-11-18.
- **A sibling of longlinelabs.com, not a twin.** The owner wants the two companies' sites to look made by the same person (`README.md`, "The brand"). Keep the shared shape; never borrow Longline's scripts, its Google fonts, or its colours.
- **The host adds nothing.** GitHub Pages, with stonefoldapp.com on Cloudflare "DNS only". The old address stonefoldlabs.com is proxied by Cloudflare only to send readers on to stonefoldapp.com, and the privacy page says so. Mail stays at `support@stonefoldlabs.com`: its records are never touched and never proxied. If hosting ever changes, `scripts/check-live.sh` must still pass and the privacy page must still be true.
- **The privacy page is true or it is changed.** If anything about hosting, mail or the app changes what we know about a person, the page changes first, with its date.

## The shape of the site (the owner, 2026-11-15)
- **One company, one app.** On 2026-11-13 the owner decided to make one app, Stonefold, from the five the site used to list. On 2026-11-15 he asked for the site to match: "a really cool way to show what the app is capable of while highlighting the encryption, privacy, and security focus of it."
- **Home is the app.** (Launch branch, 2026-11-21.) In order: a centered hero (the kicker, a headline with one word in the accent colour, one line, two buttons) with a large drawing of the Stonefold window under it, standing in for a screenshot until the owner lifts that hold; four short cards (Runs locally on your device, No AI, Offline, iCloud sync if enabled) with no heading over them (the owner, 2026-11-22: heading out, "You enter it yourself" out, two titles reworded; then each card only its icon and title, and no line between it and the drawing above); "Everything in one app" with no line under it (the owner, 2026-11-22), six cards whose lines follow the app's Help on main, one for each part of the app (Calendar, Reminders, Notes, Budget, Tools, Files), leading into the guide; the reasons, as short “Because” lines (the owner, 2026-11-18); the band on encryption, privacy and security; the company, the one price and contact; a closing question. Each section opens with one big sentence and, at most, one line under it. The layout is compact and mostly without boxes (the owner, 2026-11-21: "more compact and less spread out", "get rid of so many squares"): cards are columns under a hairline, and the only boxes are the pictures and the diagram of the encrypted files. **How it works** opens the same way: a centered kicker, headline, line and buttons, with the small drawing under them, and big centered headings over its sections. **How it works** (`/how-it-works/`) says, in five numbered parts, that the app works offline, is encrypted on the device with the keys staying there, sends nothing unless iCloud sync is on, exports, and has no account, ads or tracking. **There is no Features page** (the owner, 2026-11-21: "I kind of want 2-3 pages (the main page, the guide/wiki page, and maybe one more page, how it works I guess. Can you simplify the website so that visitors don't get confused or lost?"; "Yes, do that" to the plan): the site is Home, How it works and the guide. What the Features pages said is in the guide, whose first page gives each part a line and lists its pages, and whose "How the parts work together" is the old features index's last section. Privacy and Support are the two small pages the App Store listing links to, and sit in the footer only, off the top menu (the owner, 2026-11-21). **The guide** (`/guide/`, the owner's idea of 2026-11-21: "a data heavy tutorial section ... a wiki that even users that have already downloaded the app can use") is one long page (the owner, 2026-11-21: "just have endless scrolling", after he found whole-section pages with three panes not to his liking): every topic of every section in order, a heading for each section (id = its slug) and each topic (id = section-topic), with no pager and no script of its own. Down the left is a list, in plain `details` and `summary`, with every section open on every page (nothing is remembered: that would need a cookie or storage) and a count beside each, jumping to a place on the page; on a phone the list folds into a menu at the top. The old per-topic addresses (`/guide/<section>/<topic>/`) are tiny pages that send to the topic's place (a meta refresh of 0, a link, a canonical link, no script), which `scripts/check.py` allows on those pages only. Its pages are adapted from the app's own Help (`StonefoldApp/App/*/…Help.swift`, read only), so the words match the app's. There are no per-app pages and no app gets its own domain. The ideas for this shape came from ProjectionLab, Notion and Thunderbird (`docs-notes/2026-11-21-site-notes.md`); nothing was copied.
- **Every page carries** the Stonefold mark beside the wordmark, and a top menu of two: How it works and Guide. The footer holds How it works, the guide, Security (the band on Home), About, Privacy and Support. **There is no rail** since 2026-11-21: it led to the Features pages, which are gone, and the guide's own tree does that job. The simple line drawings made for the site (not the app's icon or its symbols) stay on Home and How it works.
- **Pictures of the app.** Until the app's own pictures are made, each section's picture is a drawing: shapes only, no words, in a `<figure class="slot" id="picture-…">`. A real picture replaces the `<svg class="drawing">` in that figure with an `<img>` and keeps a caption. Pictures are `.jpg` files in `docs/pictures/`, from made-up data only, light appearance, normal text size. Each is run through `scripts/strip-jpeg.py` first (`scripts/check.py` refuses a `.jpg` that carries Exif, XMP or a comment, or sits outside `docs/pictures/`). Look at every one before it goes up: no real names, no old app name, no price, no "LLC", no stand-in icon, no warning left over from a test. Each gets `alt` text saying what is on screen. The pictures wanted are listed in `README.md`. They come from the app's build chat; never build or run the app from here.
- **The store link arrives with the release.** Until then the page says the app is still being built, with no link. On release it gets one link to its App Store page (iPhone and Mac are planned as one store record; confirm against Apple's page when the listing exists), and `scripts/check.py` is loosened for that wording only.
- `bellwetherbudget.com` was meant to forward here. Whether it still should is the owner's to say.

## How to work
1. Plan first for anything beyond a wording fix, and wait for the owner's yes.
2. `python3 -I scripts/check.py` must say `clean` before every commit. A new outside link is added to `OUTSIDE_LINKS` there on purpose, not to quiet the check. The same goes for a word the check refuses: change the rule only when the house rules have changed.
3. After a push, run `scripts/check-live.sh`.
4. Look at every changed page in a browser, wide and at phone width, light and dark, before saying it is done.
5. Pushing, DNS changes and anything in the owner's GitHub, Cloudflare or Proton accounts are his to approve, one at a time.
6. Don't write outside this folder. The app folders beside it are read-only from here.
7. The owner is new to web hosting: give exact clicks for anything he does himself.
8. The owner asked on 2026-11-13 that Sonnet write the words. A Sonnet subagent drafts from a brief of allowed facts, and every sentence is then checked against the rules above before it goes in.

## Adding the app's policy and help at its release
Its privacy policy becomes a section of `docs/privacy/index.html` and its help a section of `docs/support/index.html`, each under a heading with the app's name and `id="stonefold"` (so the listing can link to `…/privacy/#stonefold`). The words come from the app's approved draft and must match the copy inside the app. The App Store URLs are `https://stonefoldapp.com/privacy` and `https://stonefoldapp.com/support`.
