On the Mac

The keyboard, the command box, panels and your own layout.

Today, and the status line

The day across everything you keep, and a line along the foot of the window that counts it.

Today

With two or more sections kept, Today is a button with a sun in the window’s toolbar, beside the lock, and in View ▸ Today (⌃⌘T). It shows the day across what you keep: today’s and tomorrow’s events, the reminders that are late and due today, the notes you changed last, and the budget’s Safe to Spend with the bills that wait and those coming up.

  • Double-click a row, or choose it and press Return, to open it in its own section.
  • Click a reminder’s circle, or choose it and press Space, to mark it done. Edit ▸ Undo takes that back.
  • Each part lists eight rows and says how many more there are.
  • Settings ▸ Layout ▸ Today chooses which parts Today shows, which column each is in, and their order. A part you leave out can still be a panel. Standard Today puts it back as it was.

Today only reads what you keep: looking at it changes nothing and keeps nothing. A budget or notes locked with their own passphrase say only that they show when unlocked. If Today was on screen when you last used the app, it opens on Today.

The status line

The status line along the foot of the window counts the day: events today and the next one still to start, reminders due and late, notes, and bills waiting. Click one to go to its section. View ▸ Hide Status Line puts it away, and this Mac remembers.

The pane at the right

The pane at the right of a section stays as you leave it: the calendar’s day, with the chosen event’s place and notes under the list; the reminders’ inspector; the budget’s inspector. ⌥⌘I shows or hides it, and this Mac remembers.

Panels, and your own layout

Put the parts you use beside each other, dock them where you like, and save the layout.

Showing a panel

View ▸ Panels shows a panel beside the section on screen: Events, today’s and tomorrow’s; Reminders, those late and due today; Notes, the ones you changed last; Money, the budget’s Safe to Spend and its bills. Each is Today’s part by itself, there whatever section you are in, for each section you keep.

  • Your own lists: View ▸ Panels ▸ Lists shows one of your reminders lists, a notes folder with Folders on, or a saved view of either as a panel.
  • Pictures: View ▸ Panels ▸ Pictures ▸ Add a Picture… puts a picture you choose in a panel of its own. It is kept inside your everyday file, encrypted with your calendar and reminders.
  • Charts and figures: the budget’s Home in a panel: where you stand, the month ahead, and pace through the month.
  • Sections: View ▸ Panels ▸ Sections puts a whole section beside the one on screen: the Notes beside the Calendar, say. It shows the section as you last left it in its own sidebar, and you can work in it as you would in the middle. Its menus and ⌘ keys stay with the section in the middle: to use them, click the arrows in its title bar, Show in the Middle, or press its ⌘ number. While a section is in the middle its panel waits, and it is back when another section takes the middle. Edit ▸ Undo takes back the last change made in the window, wherever it was made.

Moving and docking

Drag a panel’s name to move it, and the place it would go lights up. Near the left, the right or the foot of the section on screen, it docks there. Over another panel’s name, it joins it as a tab. Over the rest of one, it goes beside it. Let go anywhere else and nothing moves. Control-click a title bar to dock it on the left, on the right or along the bottom.

Collapsing and resizing

The pin in a title bar collapses the panel to a strip at its edge, where its name runs along the window. Click the name to slide the panel out over the section; click the section, or press Esc, to put it back. The × closes a panel, and View ▸ Panels shows it again. Drag the line between a panel and the section to make it wider or narrower.

Keys

  • Hide Panels (⌃⌘P) puts them all away and brings them back.
  • Next Panel (⌃⌘]) and Previous Panel (⌃⌘[) move the keyboard from the section to each panel in turn.
  • In a panel, ↑ and ↓ choose a row, Return or a double-click opens it in its own section, and Space marks a reminder done.

Your own layout

This Mac keeps the layout, and the app opens with it next time. Save Layout keeps it as Layout 1 to 5, with the section or Today that is in the middle, for a job: choosing one from View ▸ Panels, or pressing ⌃⌥ and its number, puts its panels back and shows what it had in the middle. Forget Layout lets one go. Name Layout gives a saved layout a name of your own, up to 40 characters. A layout holds only where each panel is and what was in the middle, never what a panel shows.

Using the keyboard

On the Mac, almost everything has a key, and the command box finds the rest.

  • ⌘/ lists every key, for every section you keep, in Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts.
  • Hold ⌘ for a moment and the keys for what is on screen show until you let go.
  • ⌘K opens the command box.
  • G then a letter goes somewhere, wherever you aren’t typing.
  • ⌘1 and on switch between the sections you keep, in the order shown.
  • ⌘N is “new” in the section on screen.

The command box

Type a few letters of a command, a place, a note, a list or a setting, choose with ↑ and ↓, and press Return. Esc closes it. The box keeps no history of what you type in it.

Type a line with a day or a time, such as “dentist fri 3pm”, and it offers to add it as an event or a reminder on that day at that time. It reads the same days and times as Typed Dates in Reminders: today, tomorrow, a weekday, a date like “nov 20”, and times like 3pm or 15:30. A time with no am or pm, like “at 3”, isn’t read. Any line can also become a new note.

Two-key jumps

G, then a letter, goes somewhere wherever you aren’t typing: T Today, with two or more sections kept, C the Calendar, R the Reminders, N the Notes, B the Budget, K the Clock, F Files, S Settings, H Help. Only the sections you keep have a letter, and after the G a hint shows them. The Clock is K because C is the Calendar’s.

Moving in lists

Shown by a key, a section’s list has the keyboard: ↑ and ↓ move through it, Return opens the chosen note or reminder, and Esc goes from a note’s words back to the list. Tab moves between the sidebar and the list. ⌥⌘1 and up are the places inside a section, where it has them.

Every key

For all of them, by section, see Every keyboard shortcut.

Keys of your own

To give a command a key of your own, turn on Change Keys in Settings, under Look. Each section’s commands are listed with their keys: choose Change… and press the new keys, with ⌘ or ⌃. A key the Mac or the whole app already uses, or that another command of the same section has, is refused, and the app says why. Standard puts a key back, and turning Change Keys off puts every key back. Your keys wait until it is on again.

Every keyboard shortcut

Every key on the Mac, by section, in one place.

These are the keys the Mac app starts with, as Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts (⌘/) lists them: first the app’s own, then each section’s. Hold ⌘ for a moment and the app shows the keys for what is on screen. A key that needs a feature says which one. Only the sections you keep have keys.

A key works in its section while that section is on screen, so ⌘N is “new” in whichever one you are in. With Change Keys on in Settings, under Look, you can give any command in a section a key of your own. See Using the keyboard.

Everything in the window

Settings, features, the menus and the toolbar, all reachable without leaving the window.

What the app’s own menus and Settings hold is in the window too, and the menus and Settings (⌘,) are where they always were.

Settings in the window

The gear at the foot of the rail, or at the foot of the sidebar when the rail is off, shows Settings in the window, where a section would be: the same groups and switches as the Settings window, in the same order. View ▸ Settings in the Window (⌃⌘,) shows it too. Choose a section or Today to go back, or the button at its head that says where you were.

Features

Features, at the foot of a section’s sidebar, shows that section’s own switches. Turned on, each adds what it does where it belongs. Turned off, it hides it and touches nothing you keep. With the Budget kept beside the Calendar or the Reminders, the switch that shows bills on the calendar and in Reminders is there too. All Settings shows the rest.

The toolbar

  • The magnifying glass opens the command box (⌘K).
  • View holds the View menu: Today, the status line, the panels and their layouts, the text size and the calculator.
  • Menus holds everything in the menus of the section on screen, as the menu bar has them at that moment, keys and all, then what File, Edit, Window and Help hold beyond the Mac’s own items, and last the section’s Features.

Three things that had only a menu item have a button of their own: the Reminders’ Inspector, New Note beside the notes’ search field, and the Budget’s Add Transaction on every screen but All Accounts and an account’s, where + adds a row to the list itself.
