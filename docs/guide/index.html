Guide

How everything in Stonefold works, one page at a time. It is written for people who already have the app, and for anyone deciding whether to.

Getting started

Pick the parts you want, then the lock, backups, export and iCloud sync.

  • Choosing what to keep

    Turn on the parts of Stonefold you want, and leave the rest off.

  • How the parts work together

    Two switches let bills, reminders and the calendar show in each other’s places.

  • How the app looks

    Choose a theme, a typeface, and how tightly things are set.

  • Settings, group by group

    Where everything is in Settings, in order.

  • Adding from the Home Screen and Shortcuts

    Start a new event, reminder, note or purchase without opening the app first.

  • The lock

    One unlock opens the app, and it locks itself when you leave.

  • A passphrase on the budget or the notes

    Add a lock of your own to the budget, the notes, or both.

  • The recovery sheet

    One printed page that gets you back in if keys are lost or a passphrase is forgotten.

  • Backups, and saving a copy

    Stonefold backs up each file once a day, and you can save an encrypted copy anywhere.

  • A new Mac or a new iPhone

    How to bring what you keep to a new device.

  • Leaving with your things

    Export Everything writes what you keep into one folder, in formats other apps open.

  • What stays private, and what leaves

    What the app keeps, and the short list of ways anything leaves your device.

  • iCloud sync

    Keep your devices matching, with nothing of yours readable on the way.

Calendar

Events, a week and a year view, alerts, templates and US holidays.

  • Adding an event

    Put an event on a day, with a time or all day.

  • Moving around the calendar

    Get to any day, week or month, and back to today.

  • The year at a glance

    Twelve months on one screen, shaded by how full each day is.

  • Alerts for events

    Have an event tell you when it is time.

  • More for the calendar

    The switches that add repeats, alerts, more calendars and more.

  • Tags and templates in the calendar

    On the Mac: group events by a word, and start new ones from a template.

  • Copying from Apple’s Calendar

    Bring the events you already have into Stonefold, once, when you choose to.

  • Your other calendars

    See Apple’s calendars beside your own, without copying anything.

  • US Holidays

    The federal holidays and a few more, worked out by the app itself.

  • Bringing in a calendar file

    Add a team’s schedule or a school’s term dates from a file ending in .ics.

  • Deleted events

    Deleted events wait in Recently Deleted until you empty it.

Reminders

Reminders as lists or boards, habits, and logs for your home and car.

  • Adding a reminder

    Write down what to remember, with a day and a time if you want them.

  • Today, and marking done

    What is due now, from every list, and how to tick it off.

  • Lists

    Keep reminders in lists, and move them between lists.

  • Alerts for reminders

    Done and Snooze, right on the alert.

  • More for reminders

    The switches that add repeats, typed dates, flags and early alerts.

  • Tags in reminders

    On the Mac: group reminders by a word, and start lists from a template.

  • Saved views in your reminders

    On the Mac: keep a list or a tag as a named view in the sidebar.

  • Habits

    Check off what you want to do regularly, and look back at how it’s going.

  • Home and car maintenance logs

    Write down what was done to your home or your car, and see what is due next.

  • A list as a board

    See a reminders list as cards in columns.

  • Copying from Apple’s Reminders

    Bring the lists you already have into Stonefold, once, when you choose to.

  • Deleted reminders

    Deleted reminders wait in Recently Deleted until you empty it.

Notes

Notes, a journal, recipes, logins, a family binder and sign-in codes.

  • Writing a note

    Start typing. There is nothing to save.

  • Finding a note

    Search looks through every word of every note as you type.

  • Folders, checklists and formatting

    The switches that add more to a note.

  • How formatting is written

    Checklists and formatting are plain marks in your own text.

  • Your notes as files

    Export your notes as plain text files, or bring a folder of them in.

  • Tags and templates

    Group notes by a word, and start new ones from a template.

  • Saved views in your notes

    On the Mac: keep a search, a tag or a folder as a named view.

  • Kinds of note

    Pick Journal, Recipe, Logins, Binder or Authenticator when you make a note.

  • Journal

    One entry for each day, with a mood if you like.

  • Recipes

    Recipes you can cook from, with amounts that scale.

  • Logins

    Keep usernames and passwords in a note, with no autofill and no connection to the internet.

  • The password generator

    Make a password or a PIN on your device, with no internet.

  • A binder for your family

    A note for what your family would need to know, with a PDF to print.

  • Sign-in codes (Authenticator)

    Keep the codes for two-step sign-in in a note, and see each one as it changes.

  • The keyboard in your notes

    Spelling, Writing Tools and what the keyboard learns.

  • VoiceOver in your notes

    Every button and field has a name VoiceOver reads.

  • Deleted notes

    Deleted notes wait in Recently Deleted until you empty it.

Budget

A budget, net worth, debt payoff and a retirement planner.

  • Adding a purchase

    Type an amount and a payee, check the category, and add it.

  • Bills and paydays ask first

    Nothing is recorded by itself. A bill waits for your one tap.

  • Where you stand, and safe to spend

    Home’s gauge says whether the month is in good shape, tight or short, and shows its working.

  • Checking a balance with your bank

    The app never connects to your bank, so you compare the two yourself.

  • The plan, and changing it

    What each category gets, this month and the months ahead.

  • Working in the grid

    On the Mac: an account’s register and the Plan are grids you edit in place.

  • Copying and pasting rows

    On the Mac: copy rows to a spreadsheet, or paste rows in, always shown first.

  • More for the budget

    The switches that add envelopes, income planning, reports and more.

  • Net worth

    What you own, what you owe, and the difference, with a chart over one, two or three years.

  • Debt payoff

    Compare ways to pay off what you owe, and see how long each takes.

  • The retirement planner

    See how a plan holds up, year by year, with your own numbers.

  • The sample budget

    Look around a made-up household before you set up your own.

  • Budgeting words

    Every word the budget uses, in plain English, with an example.

Clock and calculator

Timers, alarms, a stopwatch, countdowns, a focus timer, and calculators for sums, loans and units.

  • The stopwatch

    Time something, mark laps, and see which was quickest.

  • Timers

    Run as many timers as you like, side by side.

  • Alarms

    Set an alarm once or on the days you choose.

  • The world clock

    See the time in other cities beside your own.

  • Countdowns

    Count the days to a date, once or every year.

  • The focus timer

    Work in stretches with breaks, and see how many you finished today.

  • The calculator

    A small calculator that keeps nothing of yours.

  • The mortgage calculator

    Work out a payment and what extra payments save, with a rate you type.

  • The car loan calculator

    Compare 36, 48, 60 and 72 months for the car you are thinking of.

  • Converters

    Units, dates, tips and amounts at a rate you type.

  • The Clock beside your work

    On the Mac: timers, the stopwatch, the world clock and the calculator as panels.

  • Deleting in the Clock

    A deleted alarm, timer, city or countdown waits in the Clock’s Recently Deleted.

Files

Documents kept encrypted, with scanning on the iPhone.

  • What Files is

    A private place for your documents, inside Stonefold.

  • Bringing files in

    Drag them in, add them, send them from another app, or scan paper with the camera.

  • Folders, names and details

    Keep documents in folders, and say what each one is and when it expires.

  • Finding a file

    Search by name, by the words inside, and sort the list.

  • Looking at a file

    A preview that is drawn from memory, so no copy is written anywhere.

  • Reminders before a file expires

    An alert before a document runs out, that never names the file.

  • Deleting in Files

    Deleted files wait in Files’ own Recently Deleted.

On the Mac

The keyboard, the command box, panels and your own layout.

  • Today, and the status line

    The day across everything you keep, and a line along the foot of the window that counts it.

  • Panels, and your own layout

    Put the parts you use beside each other, dock them where you like, and save the layout.

  • Using the keyboard

    On the Mac, almost everything has a key, and the command box finds the rest.

  • Every keyboard shortcut

    Every key on the Mac, by section, in one place.

  • Everything in the window

    Settings, features, the menus and the toolbar, all reachable without leaving the window.
