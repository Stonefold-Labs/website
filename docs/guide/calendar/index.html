Adding an event

Put an event on a day, with a time or all day.

On the Mac

  1. Select a day and press ⌘N.
  2. Type the event’s title and press Return.

The selected day’s events are listed on the right. Double-click one to change it, or select it and press Return.

On the iPhone

  1. Tap +, on the month or on a day.
  2. Type the event’s title and tap Add.

From the month the event is on today. From a day, it is on that day. Tap a day to see its events in order, and tap an event to change it.

The form

A new event is all day until you turn All day off. The form also has Place, Starts, Ends and Notes.

Moving around the calendar

Get to any day, week or month, and back to today.

On the Mac

  • The arrow keys move by a day and by a week.
  • ⌘[ and ⌘] go to the month before and after, and ⌘T comes back to today.
  • Today’s number is in a filled circle. A day shows up to three titles, then how many more. The day’s list on the right has them all.

On the iPhone

  • Swipe the month to go to the one before or after. Today comes back to today.
  • Today’s number is in a filled circle. A day has a dot for each of up to three events. Tap the day to see them all.
  • At the largest text sizes the month is a list of its days, with every title.

The year at a glance

Twelve months on one screen, shaded by how full each day is.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Calendar, turn on Year.

  • On the Mac, Year in the sidebar, or ⌥⌘4, shows the twelve months of the selected day’s year. ⌥⌘[ and ⌥⌘] go to the year before and after, and ⌘T comes back to today.
  • On the iPhone, Year on the month shows the twelve months of its year. The arrows at the top go to the year before and after.

Reading it

Today’s number is in a filled circle. A day with something on it is shaded, today too: lightly for one thing, more for two or three, most for four or more. Events, reminders shown on the calendar and bills each count.

  • On the Mac, click a day to see its events in the day’s list on the right. Double-click a day, or click a month’s name, to open that month.
  • On the iPhone, tap a month to open it. At the largest text sizes the year is a list of its months, each with how many days have something on.

Alerts for events

Have an event tell you when it is time.

How do I add an alert?

  1. In Settings ▸ Features: Calendar, turn on Alerts.
  2. In an event’s form, choose Alert: at the time of the event, or before it.

For an all-day event the alert is at nine in the morning, on the day or before. The first time you choose an alert, the system asks whether the app may alert you, unless it has asked already.

What an alert shows

  • An event’s alert has one button, Snooze for 10 Minutes, which brings it back ten minutes later.
  • An alert shows the event’s title, never its place or notes. With “Show the words in alerts” off, in Settings under Alerts, it says just “Event”.
  • An alert is set on your device and rings from it. Nothing is sent to bring it.

More for the calendar

The switches that add repeats, alerts, more calendars and more.

Settings ▸ Features: Calendar turns on more: Repeating, Alerts, More calendars, Week, Year, Show my other calendars and Time zones. Each is off until you turn it on, apart from US Holidays, which is there too and starts on. On the Mac, Features at the foot of the sidebar shows the same switches.

Repeating events

With Repeating on, changing one day of a repeating event asks Change Only This One or Change This and All Later, and deleting it asks Delete Only This One or Delete This and All Later.

Reminders and bills on the calendar

  • With the Reminders kept too, Reminders on the Calendar, in the same place, shows a reminder that has a day on that day, under the day’s events, until it is done.
  • With the Budget kept too, a switch in the same place shows its bills on their due days, with their amounts and whether they are paid.

Turning a switch off

Turning a feature off hides what it adds and changes nothing in your calendar. An event that repeats keeps repeating.

Tags and templates in the calendar

On the Mac: group events by a word, and start new ones from a template.

Tags

Turn on Tags in Settings ▸ Features: Calendar, then write a word with # in front of it in an event’s title or notes, such as #garden. The sidebar lists every tag with how many events hold it. Choose one to see what is coming that holds it, for a year ahead. Double-click one to see it on its day.

Templates

  1. Turn on Templates in Settings ▸ Features: Calendar. Any event that holds #template in its title or notes is a template.
  2. Choose a day, then Calendar ▸ New Event from Template… (⌥⌘N), and pick one.

The new event has the template’s title, place, notes, calendar, times and alert, without the #template, on the day you chose. It doesn’t repeat. The template stays as it is, and Edit ▸ Undo takes the new event back.

Copying from Apple’s Calendar

Bring the events you already have into Stonefold, once, when you choose to.

How do I do it?

  1. Open Settings ▸ Bring Mine Over: Calendar and choose Bring Mine Over….
  2. The screen asks first. Choose Continue, and the system asks whether the app may use your calendars. Answer no, and nothing is read or copied.
  3. Choose which of Apple’s calendars to copy from, and the day to start from. The screen says how many events will come before anything is copied.
  4. Choose Bring Over.

What comes over

Titles, places, notes, days and times come over, and repeating events whose way of repeating this app has. One that repeats another way is left out and named afterwards, so you can add it by hand. Alerts, invitations and attachments stay behind.

Afterwards

Nothing stays linked. Nothing in Apple’s Calendar is changed, and a change made there afterwards does not show here. Doing it again copies the events again.

Your other calendars

See Apple’s calendars beside your own, without copying anything.

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Features: Calendar, turn on Show my other calendars. Your calendars in Apple’s Calendar on this device are drawn beside these, in their own colors. The first time you turn it on, the system asks whether the app may use your calendars.

Using them

  • On the Mac, they are read only. Double-click one in the day’s list to see it. It can be changed only in Apple’s Calendar. Hide one under On this Mac in the sidebar.
  • On the iPhone, they are read only. Tap one to see it. It can be changed only in Apple’s Calendar. Hide one in Calendars, in the menu on the month.

What it means for privacy

They stay where they are, protected as Apple protects them. Nothing of theirs is copied into this app’s files, changed or sent, and this app’s encryption is for its own calendar only.

If reading them is off

Settings says so and has Open Settings. Turning the feature off stops reading them. The permission itself is in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Calendars on the Mac, and in the iPhone’s Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Calendars.

US Holidays

The federal holidays and a few more, worked out by the app itself.

US Holidays come with the app: the eleven federal holidays, and Valentine’s Day, Easter Sunday, Mother’s Day, Father’s Day, Halloween, Christmas Eve and New Year’s Eve. They show from the start, all day, in their own color.

Observed days

When a federal holiday falls on a Saturday, the Friday before shows it again, “observed”: that is the federal day off. When it falls on a Sunday, the Monday after does.

What they are

They are read only and set no alerts. The app works them out itself, with nothing fetched, and they are in no export, because they aren’t yours.

How do I hide them?

Turn off US Holidays in Settings ▸ Features: Calendar. With More calendars on, you can also hide them on this device alone, under Calendars in the Mac’s sidebar or in Calendars, in the menu on the month, on the iPhone.

Bringing in a calendar file

Add a team’s schedule or a school’s term dates from a file ending in .ics.

How do I do it?

  1. Save the calendar file from your browser. Its name ends in .ics.
  2. On the Mac, choose Calendar ▸ Bring In a Calendar File…, or the same button in Settings ▸ Bring In: Calendar File. On the iPhone, tap Bring In a Calendar File… in Settings ▸ Bring In: Calendar File.
  3. Choose the file. The screen says how many events will come in before anything is copied.
  4. With More calendars on, choose Into: a new calendar named from the file, or one of yours.
  5. Choose Bring In. Undo takes it all back.

What comes in

Titles, places, notes, days and times come in, and repeating events whose way of repeating this app has. One that repeats another way is left out and named afterwards. Alerts and invitations stay behind. A link in the file comes in as words in the notes, and nothing opens it.

When the schedule changes

Nothing stays linked: the file is read once, when you choose it, and nothing is fetched. Save the new file and bring it into the same calendar. Events brought in before are brought up to date, not added twice: a game moved to another day or time moves. One the file now says is cancelled, or one still to come that the newer file no longer holds, goes to Recently Deleted.

Deleted events

Deleted events wait in Recently Deleted until you empty it.

A deleted event waits in Recently Deleted until you empty it. Nothing is removed after a number of days. Choose it there to put it back as it was.

Undo

  • On the Mac, Edit ▸ Undo takes back the last change.
  • On the iPhone, Undo, on the month and on a day, takes back the last change. So does shaking the iPhone.

Wiping

Delete Now wipes one thing, and Empty wipes everything there, days and times too. Each asks first. What is wiped can’t be brought back. Backups made before then still hold it until they age out.
