Guide

How it works

Your things stay on your iPhone, iPad, and Mac, encrypted, and the app works offline. If you want your iPhone, iPad, and Mac to match, you can turn on iCloud sync.

It works offline.

Everything you add is saved on your device first. There’s nothing to sign in to and nothing to wait for.

Alerts are set on your device and ring from it. Only iCloud sync uses the internet, and only if you turn it on.

Everything is encrypted on your device.

Your things are stored in encrypted files, and the keys stay on your iPhone and Mac. We run no server for the app, so we can’t see what’s in it.

Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode opens the app. By default, anyone who can unlock your device can open it. You can lock the budget or the notes with a passphrase of your own.

The recovery sheet, a page you print, is the way back in if a device’s keys are lost. If you lose your iPhone and Mac and the sheet, nobody can open what you kept, including us.

Nothing leaves your device unless you turn on iCloud sync.

Out of the box, nothing leaves your device. Sync is a switch in Settings, and it starts off.

Turn it on and each change is sealed on your device first, with a key only your iPhone and Mac hold. It then travels through your own iCloud to your other iPhone or Mac. Sync is end-to-end encrypted, and we run no servers.

Apple can see that your Apple Account is syncing, and when, because sync runs through iCloud.

Take your data out any time.

Your data belongs to you. Export Everything, in Settings, writes what you keep into a folder you choose, whenever you want. It comes out in everyday, open formats that other apps read easily, so you are never locked into Stonefold, and we hold no copy of what you keep.

  • .icsCalendar one file any calendar app opens
  • .csvReminders a row for each, for spreadsheet apps
  • .md or .txtNotes a plain text file for each note
  • .txtFlashcards plain text that Anki brings in
  • .xlsxBudget one workbook for spreadsheet apps
  • as they areFiles every document, in the same folders

Nothing in that folder is encrypted, so Stonefold asks first.

No account, no ads, no tracking, no AI.

Anything Stonefold works out is plain arithmetic, and it can show how.

Getting started

Choosing what to keep

How do I turn a part on or off?

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Go to Sections.
  3. Switch a part on or off: Calendar, Reminders, Notes, Budget, Tools or Files.

Welcome keeps every part the first time you open the app: the Calendar, Reminders, Notes, Budget, Tools and Files. A part that is off is hidden. Nothing you put in it is touched, and it is all there when you turn it on again. Nothing is made until you go in from the first launch’s page: then every part kept but the Budget is started, its encrypted file made, and the Budget waits for its own setup. A part you turn on later starts the first time you use it.

Extras

Each part has switches of its own, on its own page under Settings ▸ Sections once the part is started. When a part is first started, every one of them is on but Show my other calendars, which reads your own calendars on this device and asks first. Turn off any you don’t want. A part you used before keeps its switches as you left them.

The first time you open it

After Welcome, one page offers the look: each theme, font, way of setting figures and density drawn as it looks, with text size on the Mac, and a sample that changes as you choose. Start goes into the app. Skip goes in with the look as it was. Everything on the page is in Settings ▸ Look too, and Set Up Again brings the page back: on the Mac in the Help menu, on the iPhone in Settings under Help and About. Going in from it again starts nothing.

Nothing asks for permission when the app first opens. The system asks to allow alerts the first time something wants one: an event given an alert, a reminder given a time, an alarm or a timer set, or a document given an expiry day.

How the parts work together

Each part of Stonefold keeps its own encrypted file, and two switches in Settings let the parts share what they know. Both are on from the start, and you can turn either off.

Bills on the calendar

Each bill shows on its due day, with its amount. The switch is Bills on the Calendar and in Reminders, in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Budget.

Bills in Reminders

With the same switch on, waiting bills sit at the top of Today. Choose Paid and it is recorded in the budget. See Bills and paydays ask first.

Reminders on the calendar

With Reminders on the Calendar on, in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Calendar, a reminder with a day shows under that day’s events, until it is done. See More for the calendar.

How the app looks

Where do I change it?

In Settings ▸ Look. It changes how the app looks, and nothing in it.

Theme

Editor, which the app starts in, is slate, as a code editor is, with soft light words, a clear blue, and code and charts in several colors. Sage is a deep green with sage, its headings in a book face. Linen is its light page, with deep green. Night is near black with a clear blue. Ember is a warm near black with ochre. Paper is light, the color of paper, with deep teal. Dusk is a deep violet with lavender. Clay is warm plaster with terracotta. Plain is the system’s own colors, light or dark as the device is set.

Choosing a theme also sets the font, the figures and the density to the theme’s own. Each can then be changed by itself.

Font and figures

Font is one of the faces that come with the device: System, Rounded, Serif or Monospace. Figures sets amounts, dates and times in a monospace face, or in the text’s own face with digits of one width. Either way, columns of amounts line up.

Code Cursor

Code Cursor sets the insertion point in a Code note: a Line, as everywhere else, or a Block one character wide, as a terminal draws it.

On the Mac

  • Density sets how tightly rows and the room between things are set. Compact puts more in sight. Roomy gives more room.
  • Text Size makes the words of the whole window smaller or larger. The menus stay as the Mac draws them. A note keeps its own size, set from Notes ▸ Bigger Text.
  • Tabs puts the window’s tabs along the top, or down the left Side. See Tabs and desks.
  • App Icon is Standard, Match My Theme, which follows the theme, or one theme’s own. On the Mac it starts on Match My Theme. The Dock shows it while the app is open.

On the iPhone

  • Density sets the room between groups. Compact never makes a row shorter, so every row is still easy to touch.
  • The size of the words is the one you choose in the iPhone’s own Settings.
  • App Icon is Standard, Match My Theme, or one theme’s own. It is the app’s icon on the Home Screen, and the iPhone tells you each time it changes.

These choices are this device’s own settings. They are not in an export, or in the app’s own backups.

Settings, page by page

Settings opens on a short first page of rows, each opening a page of its own, on the iPhone and the Mac. On the Mac the same pages run down the side. A page shows only what has something to show: a part’s switches and alerts once the part is started, a file’s backups once the file is there.

  1. Look. Theme, font, figures, Code Cursor and density, and on the Mac the text size and the tabs. The app icon, on the Mac Change Keys, and the order of the sections, with on the Mac Today’s parts and on the iPhone which sections are on the tab bar.
  2. Sections. Which parts you keep, and the calculator’s switches. Each part you keep has a page of its own switches under it. The Budget’s holds the switch for bills on the calendar and in Reminders.
  3. The Lock.
  4. Alerts, while a part you keep sets them: the reminders’, the calendar’s and the budget’s bill reminders. Notes set no alert.
  5. Backups and Export. Backups and saved copies, by file, Export Everything and each part’s own export, and Bring Mine Over for the Calendar and the Reminders.
  6. Privacy. What Leaves This Device, and Where Your Things Are Kept.
  7. Help and About. Help, Budgeting Words while the Budget is kept, and Acknowledgements.

Under each switch, one line says what it does. The switch for iCloud sync is in Settings too. The command box opens the page that holds a setting. On the Mac the same pages are in the window as well as in the Settings window. See Everything in the window.

Adding from the Home Screen and Shortcuts

On the iPhone

  1. Touch and hold the app’s icon on the Home Screen.
  2. Choose a button. There is one for each part you keep: an event, a reminder, a note and a purchase.
  3. The app opens where you add that thing.

The Shortcuts app has one action for this app. Make a shortcut with it and choose what it starts, from the parts you keep. A shortcut can be run from Control Center, from the Action button on an iPhone that has one, or by tapping the back of the iPhone.

On the Mac

The Shortcuts app has one action for this app. Make a shortcut with it and choose what it starts: an event, a reminder, a note or a purchase, for the parts you keep. Running the shortcut opens the app where you add that thing.

Good to know

  • Neither takes anything you type. What you add is typed in the app.
  • If the app is locked, it asks to unlock first, and what you asked for opens once it is unlocked.
  • A part that is hidden has no button and is not offered to a shortcut. A part you haven’t started is only shown.

The lock

How does it work?

With Lock the app on, in Settings ▸ The Lock, opening the app asks once: Face ID or Touch ID, or the device’s passcode or password if that fails. It starts on once you keep the Notes, the Budget or Files.

By default, anyone who can unlock your device can open the app, unless you lock the budget or the notes with a passphrase.

When does it lock?

  • On the Mac, when the Mac sleeps, its screen locks, another user takes over or the window is closed, and after the time without use chosen in Settings ▸ The Lock. Lock Now is ⌃⌘L.
  • On the iPhone, after you leave it, and after a time without use, each as chosen in Settings ▸ The Lock, and at once when the iPhone itself locks.

Locked, the app shows none of your words or numbers. Until you first use a part there is nothing to lock, so it doesn’t ask.

A passphrase on the budget or the notes

How do I add one?

Neither has a passphrase unless you add one. In Settings ▸ The Lock, “Lock the budget with a passphrase” and “Lock the notes with a passphrase” each add one. With both on there is one passphrase, typed once.

If the device has Face ID or Touch ID, that still opens them day to day. When it isn’t used, the app asks for the passphrase.

If I forget it

Nobody can get a forgotten passphrase back: not us, and not Apple. It isn’t stored anywhere. While Face ID or Touch ID still opens them, Settings can turn the passphrase off and you can set a new one. Otherwise your recovery sheet, if you made one, opens what it covers. With no sheet, nothing does.

What can’t have one

The calendar and the reminders can’t have a passphrase. The app opens them itself, so that your alerts can be set. Files has no passphrase of its own either: it opens with the one unlock, with the notes and the budget.

The recovery sheet

What is it?

One page, with a code for each thing it covers. It is the way back if the device’s keys are lost, or a passphrase is forgotten.

How do I make one?

  1. The app offers it once when you first go in, for everything made then, and again when a file is made later, such as the budget’s.
  2. Or open Settings ▸ The Lock and choose Make the Recovery Sheet…, which covers whatever is open then.
  3. Once you have one, the button reads Make a New Recovery Sheet… instead.

Keeping it

Print it and keep it where you would keep a key. The app never stores a code. A new sheet stops the old one working, for everything the new one covers.

Backups, and saving a copy

Automatic backups

Once a day, when the app is unlocked, what you keep is backed up if something changed: the calendar and reminders together, the notes, and the budget, each encrypted with its own key. The newest 30 daily backups of each are kept, and 10 made with Back Up Now.

Files keeps a file of its own, backed up the same way. A backup of Files holds the list of your documents, their details, the words found in them and what opens them, not a second copy of each document. Files has no Save a Copy of its own as a whole: Save a Copy… on a document writes that document out as it is.

Putting a backup back

Settings ▸ Backups and Export puts one back. How it was is saved first, so putting one back can be undone the same way.

Save a Copy

Save a Copy… writes an encrypted copy of one file wherever you choose, sealed with a passphrase you type for that copy. A budget or notes locked with a passphrase keep that passphrase on their copies.

Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ What Leaves This Device says what a backup of the device itself holds.

A new Mac or a new iPhone

What the new device has

On a Mac, the keys to what you keep are in that Mac’s login keychain. On an iPhone, they are kept for that iPhone alone. A new iPhone set up from the old one’s backup has what you kept and not its keys. There, your recovery sheet opens it if you made one, and a budget or notes locked with a passphrase open with the passphrase.

Moving with a saved copy

  1. On the old device, use Save a Copy… for each of the calendar and reminders, the notes and the budget.
  2. On the new device, use Open a Saved Copy…, on the Welcome screen or in Settings ▸ Backups and Export.

A copy is separate. What you add on one device doesn’t appear on the other.

Moving with iCloud sync

If you use iCloud sync, a new device gets the key from one of your devices, with a code you scan or type. Your recovery sheet opens what it covers too. See iCloud sync.

Leaving with your things

Your events, reminders, notes and budget can be exported in formats other apps can open.

How do I do it?

  1. Open Settings ▸ Backups and Export.
  2. Choose Export Everything…. It says first what goes in and what is left out: a part that is hidden, not started, or waiting for its passphrase.
  3. Choose a folder. Stonefold writes one new folder inside it.

What is in the folder

  • The calendar is one .ics file of every event, with its repeats and its alert, which calendar apps can open.
  • The reminders are one CSV file, a row for each reminder in your lists, which spreadsheet apps can open.
  • The notes are a folder with a plain text file for each note, which any text editor opens. A deck’s cards are written as a file of their own, the kind Anki brings in, without pictures or your answers.
  • The budget is one .xlsx workbook, which spreadsheet apps can open: your transactions, accounts, categories and bills, with a summary of each month.
  • Files is a folder holding every document as it is, in the same folders.

On the Mac, with Saved Views on, the notes’ saved views go in a CSV file in the notes’ folder, and the reminders’ in a CSV file of their own. The pictures you put in panels go in a folder named Pictures, each a JPEG file, named for when you added it.

What is not in it

The alarms, timers and cities of Tools aren’t in the export, and the sheet says so. They are kept with the calendar and reminders, so Save a Copy… of the calendar and reminders carries them. Recently Deleted is left out. Some marks and settings stay behind: a calendar’s color, which notes are pinned, and in the budget its targets and paydays.

Keep in mind

Nothing in that folder is encrypted. Anyone who can open it can read it, so keep it as you would keep the things themselves. Each part’s own export is in Settings ▸ Backups and Export too. To move to another device, use Save a Copy… instead: a saved copy stays encrypted, and opens there as it was here.

What stays private, and what leaves

No account. No sign-up. No ads, no tracking, no analytics, and no AI.

What you keep is stored on your devices, encrypted. It works offline. We can’t see what’s in it.

What leaves your device

Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ What Leaves This Device says it in plain sentences. With sync off, nothing leaves, unless you save it somewhere yourself. With sync on, sealed changes go to your own iCloud, and Apple can see that your Apple Account is syncing, and when.

  • A saved copy stays encrypted, sealed with its passphrase.
  • An export isn’t encrypted, and the app asks before it makes one.
  • The recovery sheet is a page you print. It opens what it covers, so keep it where you would keep a key.
  • An alert is set on your device and rings from it. Nothing is sent to bring it.
  • A backup of the device itself holds what you keep here, still encrypted. Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ What Leaves This Device says what it holds.

Files

  • Your files stay on your device, each sealed by itself with a key of its own, inside the app’s encrypted storage.
  • The words inside a file are found on your device, for search: a PDF’s own text, and with Find Words in Pictures and Scans on, words in pictures, read by Apple’s Vision framework with no language model. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • A file goes out of the app only when you choose Save a Copy… or Export Everything, and then as it is, not sealed, where you choose.
  • On the iPhone, the camera is used only when you choose Scan Document, and the pages go straight into Files.
  • A reminder before a file expires says only “A document expires soon.”, never the file’s name.

iCloud sync

What it is

Sync is a switch in Settings, and it starts off. When you turn it on, each change is sealed on your device first, with a key only your devices hold. It then travels through your own iCloud to your other devices. Sync is end-to-end encrypted. Only your devices hold the key. It runs through your own iCloud, and we run no servers.

What it covers

Every section you keep, Files included: the Calendar, Reminders, Notes, Budget, Tools and Files.

What Apple can see

That your Apple Account is syncing, and when.

Adding a device

A new device gets the key from one of your devices, with a code you scan or type. Your recovery sheet opens what it covers too.

The price of this privacy

If you lose every device and every copy of the key, nobody can open what you kept, including us. Print the recovery sheet and keep it where you would keep a key.

If you don’t want it

Leave it off. Every part of Stonefold works without it, and nothing leaves your device.

Calendar

Adding an event

On the Mac

  1. Select a day and press ⌘N.
  2. Type the event’s title and press Return.

The selected day’s events are listed on the right. Double-click one to change it, or select it and press Return.

On the iPhone

  1. Tap +, on the month or on a day.
  2. Type the event’s title and tap Add.

From the month the event is on today. From a day, it is on that day. Tap a day to see its events in order, and tap an event to change it.

The form

A new event is all day until you turn All day off. The form also has Place, Starts, Ends and Notes.

Moving around the calendar

On the Mac

  • The arrow keys move by a day and by a week.
  • ⌘[ and ⌘] go to the month before and after, and ⌘T comes back to today.
  • Today’s number is in a filled circle. A day shows up to three titles, then how many more. The day’s list on the right has them all.

On the iPhone

  • Swipe the month to go to the one before or after. Today comes back to today.
  • Today’s number is in a filled circle. A day has a dot for each of up to three events. Tap the day to see them all.
  • At the largest text sizes the month is a list of its days, with every title.

The year at a glance

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Calendar, turn on Year.

  • On the Mac, Year in the sidebar, or ⌥⌘4, shows the twelve months of the selected day’s year. ⌥⌘[ and ⌥⌘] go to the year before and after, and ⌘T comes back to today.
  • On the iPhone, Year on the month shows the twelve months of its year. The arrows at the top go to the year before and after.

Reading it

  • Each entry has a Title, Details and, if you want one, a Number.
  • Change… and Delete… are on each entry. Delete asks first, and Undo brings it back.
  • In the list of notes, a binder says how many entries it has, not what is in them.

Numbers

An account, policy or member number shows as dots until you show this is your device, the same as a password. Show Number, Hide Number and Copy Number are on the entry.

Online accounts

Online accounts are named by site only. Add Sites from Your Logins adds the sites in your Logins notes by name. No password ever goes in the binder.

Making a PDF

Make a PDF… makes the binder’s pages on your device, to print or hand to someone you trust. The PDF isn’t encrypted, and it says so before it is made. Numbers show only their last four characters unless you choose Include Numbers…, which asks first that this is your device.

The lock

By default, anyone who can unlock your device can open the binder. For a lock of its own, turn on Lock the notes with a passphrase in Settings ▸ The Lock.

Code notes

How do I turn it on?

Code is on once the notes are first started. If it is off, turn it on in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Notes. On the Mac, choose Notes ▸ New Code Note. On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil and choose Code. A new one is named Untitled Code, until you change it.

What a Code note is

  • Its words are in a monospace face, colored by the language you choose from the menu above it: Swift, Python, JavaScript and TypeScript, HTML, CSS, JSON, YAML, Markdown, Shell, SQL or Plain Text. On the Mac, they have line numbers too.
  • Nothing in a Code note is ever run. It keeps the code, and that is all.
  • On the Mac, Tab puts in spaces, Shift-Tab takes them out, and Return keeps the line’s indent. On the iPhone, Return keeps the line’s indent.

Finding and copying

On the Mac, Notes ▸ Find and Replace… (⌥⌘F) finds words in a Code note and changes them. Copy All copies the whole note.

Export, and code inside other notes

Export Notes… writes a Code note as a file of its language, such as .py or .swift, and Bring In Notes… brings such a file back as a Code note.

With Simple formatting on, three backticks on a line of their own begin a block of code in any note, with a language’s name after them, and three more end it. See How formatting is written.

Flashcards

How do I turn it on?

Flashcards is on once the notes are first started. If it is off, turn it on in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Notes. Then choose Deck in New Note’s list, or on the Mac Notes ▸ New Deck. A deck holds cards, and Study lists every deck with how many cards are new, learning and due.

Adding cards

Add Card… makes a card of one of four kinds:

  • Basic has a front and a back.
  • Basic and Reversed is studied both ways, as two cards.
  • Type the Answer asks you to type the back before it is shown.
  • Cloze hides parts of a sentence. In Water boils at {{c1::100}} degrees {{c2::Celsius}}, each part between the marks is hidden on a card of its own, one for each number. A hint can follow a second ::, as in {{c1::100::a number}}.

Pictures on cards

A card can have a picture on its front, its back or both, chosen from your photos or your files, or dragged onto the card on the Mac. A picture is made smaller on the way in and keeps only its pixels: where and when it was taken, and the camera, are left behind. Give it a description, and VoiceOver reads that. Search reads a card’s words, never its pictures.

Bringing cards in, and sending them out

Bring In Cards… adds the cards in a text file to the deck: a file Anki writes with Export ▸ Notes in Plain Text, a file Quizlet exports with a tab between each term and its definition, or a spreadsheet saved as CSV. Choose the file’s pictures with it, and the cards that name them get them. Export Cards… writes the deck as a file Anki can bring in, with the pictures it names beside it. Anki’s own deck packages aren’t read.

Export Notes… and Export Everything write each deck’s cards as the same kind of file, without pictures or your answers. See Leaving with your things.

Deleting a deck

A deleted deck takes its cards, pictures and answers to Recently Deleted with it. Choose it there to put it back, and they come back too.

Studying a deck

A round of study

Study Now shows each card’s front. Show the answer, then say how it went: Again if you forgot, Hard, Good, or Easy. Each button shows when the card comes back after that answer. A new card comes back in minutes at first; once learned, in days.

When a card comes back

When a card comes back is worked out with FSRS, the method Anki uses by default, from all your answers to it: the better you know a card, the longer it waits. It aims for you to remember 90 percent of cards when they come back. Deck Settings… can change that, and how many new cards and reviews each day brings. A study day starts at 4 in the morning.

Keys

  • On the Mac, Space or Return shows the answer, 1 to 4 answer, ⌘Z takes the last answer back, and Esc ends.
  • On the iPhone, tap the card to show the answer, and Undo takes the last answer back.

When you are done

When a deck is done for the day it says so, and how many cards tomorrow brings. Stats shows today’s answers, the cards due in the next seven days, and how often you remembered learned cards over the last thirty days. On the Mac, Today and the status line say how many cards there are to study.

Authenticator notes

How do I set it up?

  1. Authenticator is on once the notes are first started. If it is off, turn it on in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Notes.
  2. On the Mac, choose Notes ▸ New Authenticator Note (⌃⌘N). On the iPhone, touch and hold the pencil and choose Authenticator.
  3. Choose Add Account…, and type or paste the key a site shows when you turn on two-step sign-in with an authenticator app.

Add Account… also has More Options: Algorithm (SHA-1, SHA-256 or SHA-512), Digits (6 or 8) and New code every (30, 60 or 15 seconds). Change these only if the site says to. Most use SHA-1, six digits and 30 seconds.

Using the codes

The note shows each account’s code as it changes, with a ring for the seconds it has left. Click or tap a code to copy it.

  • On the Mac, the app takes the code off the clipboard after a minute, or as it quits, unless you’ve copied something since. Universal Clipboard doesn’t offer it on your other devices.
  • On the iPhone, it stays on this iPhone’s clipboard for a minute, and Universal Clipboard doesn’t offer it on your other devices.

An Authenticator note opens only with the notes passphrase, as a Logins note does. Face ID and Touch ID open the notes, but not these.

The check before codes show

The first time you open an Authenticator note after unlocking the app, it asks for Touch ID or your Mac’s password (on the iPhone, Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode) before it shows the codes. If you cancel, the names still show and the codes stay hidden, with Show Codes to ask again.

Looking after your accounts

  • Edit Names… changes the site’s and your own names for an account, never its key or how its codes are made. A new key is a new account.
  • Delete Account… asks first. Before you delete one, turn off two-step sign-in at the site, or be sure you can sign in another way. Undo takes it back.
  • Your accounts are kept in the note, with your notes, encrypted. The app asks you to make a recovery sheet before the first account, and it’s wise to keep each site’s own backup codes too.
  • Search finds an Authenticator note by its name and its accounts’ names, never by a key. Export Notes… writes each account’s key as readable text, and says so before it writes anything.
  • If you turn Authenticator off, it stops being offered when you make a note, and notes already made keep opening and showing their codes.

The keyboard in your notes

  • On the Mac, spelling is corrected and checked as you type in your notes. To change that, Control-click in a note and choose Spelling and Grammar.
  • On the iPhone, spelling is corrected and checked as you type. Settings ▸ Notes: the keyboard changes that.

Apple’s Writing Tools and inline predictions are off in your notes and their search.

Apple doesn’t say that any app’s settings stop the keyboard learning, so this app doesn’t claim to.

VoiceOver in your notes

  • With Simple formatting on, VoiceOver’s rotor lists a note’s headings and links and moves from one to the next. On the Mac, press Control-Option-U. On the iPhone, turn two fingers on the screen.
  • The marks themselves (#, **, - [ ]) are read as written. How much of them you hear follows VoiceOver’s punctuation setting. On the iPhone, VoiceOver says “heading” as it reads one.
  • With Checklists on, Tick or Untick is a VoiceOver action on the note. On the iPhone, swipe up or down to it.
  • In an Authenticator note, VoiceOver hears an account’s names, then its code a digit at a time and the seconds left, with Copy Code as the hint.

Deleted notes

A deleted note waits in Recently Deleted until you empty it. Nothing is removed after a number of days. Choose it there to put it back, and you can change it again. A deleted deck goes there with its cards, pictures and answers, and comes back with them.

When you empty Recently Deleted, the words are wiped from your notes on this device. Backups made before then, Time Machine’s and iCloud’s among them, keep them until they age out.

Budget

Adding a purchase

How do I add one?

  • On the Mac, with the Budget showing, press ⌘N. Type the amount, then the payee: choose one you’ve used before from the list under the field, and it fills in the category it had last time. Check the category, then click Add.
  • On the iPhone, tap Add at the bottom of a budget screen. Type the amount, tap Next, then type the payee: tap one you’ve used before in the list under it, and it fills in the category it had last time. Check the category, then tap Add.

Money in, transfers and more

Money out is the usual kind. Choose money in or a transfer at the top. Yesterday is one tap on the sheet itself. The account, another date and a memo are under More.

Once it’s saved, the sheet says what that category has left, with Undo beside it.

Bills and paydays ask first

Nothing is recorded by itself. On its due day a bill shows under Waiting for You on Home, with Paid, Different Amount… and Not Yet. A payday asks the same way, with It Came.

One tap records the transaction with the bill’s amount, account and category. Typing the payment yourself counts just the same.

On the calendar and in Reminders

With the Calendar or the Reminders kept too, the “Bills on the Calendar and in Reminders” switch, in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Budget, shows a bill on its due day in the calendar, and with today’s reminders while it waits. Among the reminders, Paid, Different Amount… and Not Yet work as they do on Home.

Where you stand, and safe to spend

The gauge

Home’s gauge says whether the month is in good shape, tight or short: one track, red at the left for short, yellow in the middle for tight and green at the right for good shape, with a pointer above it where the month stands, and “Short” and “Good shape” under its ends. The sentence under it says the same in words. It comes from three answers: what’s safe to spend, whether this month’s bills are covered, and your pace through the month.

The further left the pointer, the worse the month. Each third of the track is one of the three words, and inside its third the pointer follows the same answers. When short, it’s further left the further below zero the money would go, against what you have now and the pay still to come this month. When tight, it’s further left when nothing is safe to spend, when bills would take more of the money set aside, or the further behind your pace is. In good shape, the plan’s day itself is the middle of its third, and spending slower moves it right.

Hiding the gauge

  • On the Mac, turn off Show the Gauge in Settings, under Home, or Control-click Where You Stand and choose Hide Gauge. It’s hidden on this Mac only, and the sentence stays.
  • On the iPhone, turn off Show the Gauge in Settings, under Home. It’s hidden on this iPhone only, and the sentence stays.

Safe to spend

Safe to spend is what your everyday categories have left this month, lowered when the plan asks for more money than there is. It never goes below zero.

Can I Afford It?

Can I Afford It? answers for one purchase. When the answer is yes but the purchase would still make the month tight or short, a second line says so.

How it was worked out

Under the gauge, click (or on the iPhone, tap) How This Was Worked Out for every answer’s sums. It’s arithmetic on what you typed, nothing more. Pace shows once there’s an everyday plan to keep pace with.

Checking a balance with your bank

  1. Open an account and choose Check Balance…. It shows what your budget says the account holds.
  2. If your bank shows the same, choose It Matches, and the account is marked as checked today.
  3. If not, choose It Doesn’t and type the bank’s figure. You’ll see the difference, and can mark recent transactions as not at the bank yet.

Still off? Add what was missed, fix a typo, or add a correction: one entry, clearly labelled.

The plan, and changing it

On the Mac

Plan is a grid: each category down the side, with what’s spent and what’s left this month, then what’s planned for each of the next twelve months, and the twelve added up. Each group’s line adds up its categories, and the line along the foot adds up them all.

Type a figure in a month to change it: it becomes that category’s amount for that month of the year, every year, as Set by Month… sets it, and this month’s also changes this month’s plan. A figure the plan would give anyway is left to the plan. With Envelope budgeting on, amounts are set on the Budget screen instead, and the months are read only. Right-click a category or a group to rename, hide or move it.

On the iPhone

Plan lists each category with its amount this month, what’s spent and what’s left. Change an amount for this month only or from now on, or set different amounts for different months of the year. With Envelope budgeting on, amounts are set on the Budget screen instead. Categories and groups can be added, renamed, hidden and moved.

A budget is meant to change from month to month. Changing it isn’t falling behind.

Working in the grid

An account’s register and Plan are grids. Click a cell, or move with the arrow keys. Tab goes along a row and on to the next. Type to replace what’s in the chosen cell, or press Return or click it again to change it as it is. While typing, Return keeps it and goes down, Tab keeps it and goes right, and Esc leaves the cell as it was.

Undo, several rows and copying down

Each change is one step of Edit ▸ Undo. Shift with the up or down arrow, or a click with Shift, chooses more rows, and the totals row along the foot then adds up just those. ⌘D copies the first chosen row’s cell down to the others.

In a register

Click a column’s heading to sort by it, again for the other way, and a third time for the register’s own order. A split’s lines, the other account of a transfer, and a row’s account are changed in the entry strip above the grid: typing in one of those cells opens it. In the cleared column, Space marks the chosen rows cleared or not. New transactions are added with ⌘N, as before.

Copying and pasting rows

Copying

In the grid, ⌘C copies the chosen rows the way a spreadsheet app takes them. They’re marked for this Mac only, so Universal Clipboard doesn’t offer them on your other devices. While they’re on the clipboard, any app on this Mac can read them, as with anything you copy.

Pasting into a register

⌘V in a register reads rows copied from a spreadsheet as new transactions, in the register’s own order of columns from Date to Inflow, or in any order when the first row names the columns. A column named Amount is one figure, below zero for money out. In All Accounts an Account column comes after Date.

Before anything is added, a sheet shows each row as it was read, and why any row can’t be kept. Choose Keep, and each row is added as a change of its own. Edit ▸ Undo takes the whole paste back. A paste never changes a transaction already there.

Pasting into the plan

⌘V in Plan puts figures in the months from the chosen cell, rightward and downward, shown first in the same way. Only the months change.

Net worth, Debt payoff and Retirement planning are switches there too. Each is on once your budget is made. With Envelope budgeting on, what your budget’s setup plans for its first month is what is assigned. On the Mac, Features at the foot of the sidebar shows the same switches.

Net worth

How do I turn it on?

Net worth is on once your budget is made, and Net Worth has a place of its own: in the Mac’s sidebar, and under More on the iPhone. If it is off, turn it on in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Budget.

What it shows

What is owned and what is owed today, each account with its balance, the totals, and a chart over one, two or three years. Accounts outside the budget are marked so. It is typed by hand, so Stonefold never connects to a bank.

Keeping values up to date

  • Update Values… lists each account outside the budget, with what it holds now and a field for its value now (for a loan, what is owed). Save writes one entry for each one changed, labelled “Value update” and dated today, in one Undo step.
  • Add an Asset… opens the new account sheet with Type already set to “Asset or Investment (tracking only)”, and Include in budget off.

A value is a correction, as a balance check’s is, so the history and the chart follow it.

Debt payoff

How do I turn it on?

Debt payoff is on once your budget is made, and Debt Payoff has a place of its own. If it is off, turn it on in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Budget.

What you enter

Each card and loan account that you owe on is listed with its balance, and has fields for its rate (APR) and its minimum. You can also type in debts by name and balance. Then set the extra you can pay each month, and the order.

The orders

  • Smallest balance first.
  • Highest rate first.
  • Your own order.

Each month interest is added and every minimum is paid. What is left of the minimums and the extra goes to the first debt in the order still owed, then the next. A debt paid off frees its minimum for the rest. The order is fixed from the starting balances and rates.

What it shows

When each debt is paid off, the interest in all, the smallest-balance and highest-rate orders side by side, and the plan month by month, starting next month. If a plan would take more than 100 years, it says “Not in 100 years”. What you type in Debt Payoff is forgotten when the app locks, unless you choose Save Plan, which keeps it in your budget. When Debt Payoff opens with nothing typed, the saved plan comes back.

Exporting the plan

Export Plan saves the plan as a spreadsheet or a CSV file, after saying the file isn’t encrypted.

Saving the plan

Save Plan keeps one plan in your own budget’s encrypted file: each account’s APR and minimum, the debts you typed in, the extra and the order. It is not offered in the sample budget. A line says where the plan stands: “Not saved”, “Saved in your budget” or “Changed since it was saved”. Go Back to the Saved Plan brings the saved one back, and Delete the Saved Plan… takes it out of your budget; what is on screen stays until the app locks.

The retirement planner

How do I turn it on?

Retirement planning is on once your budget is made. If it is off, turn it on in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Budget. The Mac’s budget sidebar has Retirement, after Reports, and the command box finds it and New Retirement Plan…. Each one is a “retirement plan”: “Plan” is already the budget’s monthly plan. Plans are kept in the budget’s file, encrypted. On the iPhone, Retirement is in the budget’s list of screens.

Starting a plan

New Retirement Plan… asks four things: the year you were born, the age you’d like to retire at, what you spend a year, and what you earn a year. It fills in the rest. It counts your budget’s own accounts, other than cards and loans, as cash, and an account you track outside the budget only if you choose it, as taxable: count investments, not a house or a car. You set each one’s type. To start it assumes an end age of 95, inflation 3%, tax 15%, returns of 6% and 2%, volatility 12% and a safe withdrawal rate of 4%. The plan’s page says what it assumed, each with Change beside it. On the Mac, click any part of the plan to change it, or + beside a list to add to it. On the iPhone, tap New Retirement Plan… to make one, tap any part of it to change it, or + beside a list to add to it.

The page

The answer comes first: whether your money lasts to 95 or runs out at an age, the chance of success, and the target and when it is met. Then a chart of net worth over your life, with retirement and milestones marked, and the plan’s parts in short lists: Accounts, Income, Spending, Saving, Milestones and Social Security. Each part is a sheet. On the Mac, By Year shows every year: age, income, Social Security, taxes, spending, saved, taken out and net worth, in today’s money or that year’s. On the iPhone, By Year shows every year’s net worth in today’s money. Change… opens More About This Plan: the ages, the rates, the withdrawal order, the leftover account, the safe rate and the number of tries.

Comparing plans

To compare, choose Duplicate from the plan’s menu, then change the copy: retiring at 60 and at 65, say. On the Mac, Compare draws up to three plans side by side.

What goes in a plan

  • Accounts: cash, taxable, tax-deferred (such as a 401(k) or traditional IRA), Roth and HSA. Each balance is read from one of your budget’s accounts, or typed, and grows at the plan’s rate for its type, or a rate of its own.
  • Streams: income, spending, and contributions into an account, by you or by an employer. Each is in today’s money and rises with prices unless you say not, from now, an age, retirement or a milestone, up to another.
  • Milestones: retiring, and any others at an age: a house, college, a windfall. Each can bring money once, and money yearly for some years.
  • Social Security: typed from your own statement: the monthly benefit at full retirement age (67 for anyone born in 1960 or later, or set yours) and the age you will claim it, 62 to 70. The claiming age changes the benefit by the Social Security Act’s rule for early and late claims. It rises with prices.
  • Taxes: one rate you type, on taxed income and Social Security less what is put in before tax, on all of a tax-deferred withdrawal, and on the growth share of a taxable one.
  • Withdrawals: when a year spends more than comes in, from the accounts in a plain order: cash, taxable, tax-deferred, Roth, HSA. You can change the order under More.

The target and the chance of success

The target is a year of retirement’s spending over a safe withdrawal rate, in today’s money, and the age at which your accounts first hold it. The chance of success is worked out from a thousand lives on your device, each drawing every year’s market return from a normal curve with the expected return as its middle and a volatility you set as its spread. Cash never moves with the markets. The same plan always gives the same chance. It is shown beside the plain projection, with the band one life in ten either side.

What it leaves out

It is an estimate, not financial advice. Today is taken as the start of this year. A year grows first, and its money comes and goes at its end. Growth isn’t taxed until it is taken out. No tax tables, early-withdrawal penalties, contribution limits or required withdrawals are counted, and the plan follows one person’s ages: a partner’s income is a stream from an age. Like everything Stonefold works out, it is plain arithmetic, with no AI.

The sample budget

Look Around a Sample Budget, offered in the Budget section before you choose Set Up Your Budget, opens a made-up household with three years of spending, bills and pay. Try anything in it.

Nothing in it is kept: it goes when you leave it, and it’s made fresh next time. Start My Own Budget takes you to a budget of your own.

Budgeting words

Each word has one short meaning and an everyday example. Words that belong to a feature only show when that feature is on.

Activity
What went in or out of a category this month; spending shows as a minus. For example: Two grocery trips of 80 and 45 make Groceries’ activity −125. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Assigned
What you give a category this month, to spend or to keep. For example: Assigning 600 to Groceries in October plans 600 of food for October. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Available
What a category has left now: what it carried in, plus what was assigned, plus its activity. For example: 600 assigned and 125 spent leave 475 available. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Budget Accounts
Accounts you budget from, such as checking, savings and cards. For example: Checking, savings and your cards: the money your budget and Safe to Spend count.
Carried In
What a category had left at the end of last month, which it keeps this month. For example: 40 left in Dining Out on September 30 starts October with 40 carried in. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Cleared
Transactions the bank has already posted. For example: A purchase on your October statement is cleared.
Due
A bill not paid yet this month, with its due day still to come. For example: A streaming service is due October 28. Until you enter its payment, it reads Due.
Features
Extra tools you can turn on in Settings, such as envelope budgeting, income planning and reports. For example: Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Budget: turn on Reports, and Reports appears in the sidebar.
Funded
A bill or target already has all the money it needs this month. For example: Internet costs 89.99 and its category holds 89.99: funded before it’s due. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Overdue
Its due day passed and no payment has been entered. For example: Water was due October 5, and by October 23 no payment has been entered.
Overspent
A category spent more than it had, so its Available is below zero. For example: Dining Out had 50 and spent 70: it’s 20 overspent until you cover it from another category. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Paid
This month’s payment for the bill is in your transactions. For example: Electric’s 188.60 payment on October 15 marks October paid.
Partial
Some of what a bill needs this month is set aside, but not all of it. For example: 50 set aside for an 89.99 bill is partial: it needs 39.99 more. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Projected End of Month
The money with no job left at the month’s end, once pay still expected arrives and the budget is spent. For example: 20,000 in cash, 500 of bills to pay, 1,000 still budgeted and 2,000 of pay to come: 20,500. Shown with Income planning on.
Reconcile
Checking your budget’s balance against a statement’s, then locking the transactions you checked. For example: The statement says 1,000 on October 9 and so does your budget: one click marks those rows checked. Shown with Advanced transactions on.
Safe to Spend
What’s left this month for everyday things and fun, after bills and money set aside for later. For example: Everyday has 420 left and Just for Fun 120: 540 is safe to spend, about 108 a day for 5 days.
Starting Balance
What an account held on its first day in your budget. For example: A card that owed 412 on July 1 starts at −412.
Target
What a category aims to have, every month or by a date, so you know how much to assign. For example: A 600-a-month Groceries target asks for 600 each month; Assign to All Targets gives it. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Tracking Accounts
Accounts you watch without budgeting from them, such as a mortgage or investments. For example: A 401(k) shows its balance and history, but isn’t divided into categories.
Transfer
Money moved between two of your accounts: not income, and not spending. For example: Paying the card from checking is a transfer: both balances change, and no category does.
True Expenses
Costs that come now and then, saved for a little each month so they don’t catch you out. For example: Car repairs, gifts, a yearly subscription: 36 a month for the car is there at its next service. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Unassigned
Money you have that isn’t planned for anything yet: give it a job by assigning it to categories. For example: You were paid 3,000 and gave 2,500 to categories: 500 is Unassigned. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Uncleared
Transactions you’ve entered that the bank hasn’t posted yet. For example: A payment you entered today, before the bank has posted it.
Unfunded
Nothing is set aside for a bill yet this month. For example: Set Aside on the Dashboard assigns what the bills due soon still need. Shown with Envelope budgeting on.
Working Balance
Cleared plus uncleared: what the account holds once everything posts. For example: 1,200 cleared + −200 uncleared = 1,000 working balance.

The stopwatch

How do I use it?

The focus timer

How do I turn it on?

In Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Tools, turn on Focus Timer. Focus is then a place in Tools, after Timers.

How does it run?

Choose Start Focus (on the Mac, ⌥⌘Return). A focus runs, then a short break, again and again, with a long break after the last focus of the set. Then it rests until you start it again.

  • Pause holds it, and Go On carries on.
  • Skip Break goes straight to the next focus, or, from the long break, ends the set.
  • Stop Focus puts it away.

It starts at 25 minutes of focus, 5 of break and a long break of 15 after every fourth. Lengths… changes them while no focus is going. A focus can be 1 to 120 minutes, a break 1 to 60, and a long break can come after every 2 to 8 focuses.

What it rings and counts

It rings at the end of each focus and each break, as a timer rings, saying only “Focus done” or “Break done”. Each focus that ran its whole length counts toward today’s sessions. It shows that count in plain words. It keeps time while the app is closed.

The calculator

Where is it?

  • On the Mac, View ▸ Calculator (⌃⌘C) opens a small window of its own. The command box finds it too. Locked, it shows only “Locked”.
  • On the iPhone, it is a sheet from More ▸ Calculator, or from Settings when you keep one section. It has a pad of keys.

How do I use it?

Type a sum: figures, +, - or −, * × or x, / or ÷, brackets and %. Return works it out and leaves the answer to go on from. Esc clears it, and Esc again closes it. In the calculator’s other modes, Esc closes the calculator. ↑ and ↓ bring back the sums before. Copy Answer (⇧⌘C on the Mac) copies the answer.

How it works things out

  • It is decimal, not binary, so 0.1 + 0.2 is 0.3.
  • × and ÷ come before + and −, from the left, with brackets first.
  • A % is per hundred, and after + or − it is a share of what comes before: 80 + 15% is 92.
  • It uses the device’s decimal mark and its thousands mark between figures.

When a sum can’t be worked out it says why: “That isn’t a sum”, “Nothing can be divided by zero.” or “The answer is too large to show.”

What it keeps

What it worked out is kept in memory only, fifty sums at most, and forgotten when the app locks. The mortgage calculator

Where is it?

Choose Mortgage in the menu at the top of the calculator, which holds Sums, Mortgage, Car Loan, Refinance, Savings Goal, Percent and Converters. Every rate is one you type: nothing is looked up, and no rates come with the app. What you type is forgotten when the app locks, unless you save it as a scenario.

What you enter

  • The price, and the down payment as an amount or a percent.
  • The interest rate in percent a year, the term in years, and the month of the first payment. It is next month unless you choose another. 15 and 30 years are shown side by side.
  • Property tax a year, home insurance a year, PMI in percent a year, and HOA dues a month, if you have them.
  • Extra payments: a monthly amount, a yearly amount, which is paid with every twelfth payment, and one amount once, at a payment number.

Typed fields are read as sums, so “250,000” and “250000 × 0.2” both work, with or without a currency sign, and a rate’s “6.5%” is 6.5 percent. Each extra goes to what you owe.

  • The monthly payment, broken down into principal and interest, tax, insurance, PMI and HOA.
  • The payoff month, the interest in all, and what the extra payments save in interest and in months.

The refinance calculator

The savings goal calculator

Files

What Files is

Files keeps the documents you’d rather not lose: IDs, insurance, receipts, warranties and manuals. It is a place inside the app, not a cloud drive.

How do I turn it on?

Welcome keeps Files, as it keeps every part, and Settings ▸ Sections turns it off and on. Once Files is kept, the app’s lock is on, as it is for the notes.

How is it kept?

  • Files keeps a file of its own, encrypted, opened by the same unlock as the notes and the budget.
  • Each document is sealed by itself, with a key of its own, inside that encrypted storage on this device.
  • Nothing in Files is copied into your other files, a setting, an alert or a log.

The recovery sheet covers Files too, with a code of its own. Backups of Files hold the list of your documents and what opens them, not a second copy of each document.

Bringing files in

On the Mac

  • Drag files from the Finder onto the list, or onto a folder in the sidebar.
  • Or choose Files ▸ Add Files… (⌘N).
  • A folder dragged in comes in as a folder, with what is in it.

On the iPhone

  • Tap Add, then Add Files to choose files.
  • Or tap Add, then Scan Document to scan pages with the camera. A scan is kept as one PDF. The first time you scan, the system asks whether the app may use the camera. The camera is used for nothing else.
  • From another app, choose this app among the places a file can be sent, and the file comes into Files. The system copies it into the app’s own folder, where it waits, unreadable while the iPhone is locked and left out of its backups, until it is sealed into Files and that copy is deleted.

What can come in

Any kind of file. Each file is sealed by itself, with a key of its own, inside the app’s encrypted storage on this device. A file may be up to 2 GB.

Folders, names and details

Folders and names

  • On the iPhone, Add ▸ New Folder makes a folder inside the one on screen. Swipe a row to delete it. A file’s own screen has Move To and Rename….

A folder can’t go into itself or into a folder inside it. Deleting a folder sends what is in it to Recently Deleted with it.

Details

A file’s details say what kind it is: ID, Insurance, Receipt, Warranty, Manual or Other. If you set them, they also say the day it was purchased and the day it expires. A file within 30 days of expiring says “Expires soon”.

Finding a file

Search

Search finds a file by its name, and by the words inside it: a PDF’s own text, a text file’s words, and, with Find Words in Pictures and Scans on, the words in pictures and scans. Every word you type must be in the name or in the words found. Capitals and accents don’t matter.

Words in pictures

Words in pictures are read on this device by Apple’s Vision framework, with no language model. Nothing is sent anywhere. The switch is in Settings ▸ Sections ▸ Files, and is on once Files is first started. While it is on, it looks through the files it hasn’t looked at yet, and says so under the switch.

Sorting

  • On the Mac, click a heading of the list to sort by it. Click it again to turn the order round. You can sort by name, date added, kind, expiry date or size.
  • On the iPhone, Add ▸ Sort By changes the order of the list.

Folders come first.

Looking at a file

A PDF, a picture or a text file is shown in its details, drawn from memory: no copy is written anywhere to show it. The app’s own preview opens a file of up to 200 MB. A larger one offers Save a Copy… instead.

Another kind of file shows its name, kind and size, and can be saved out with Save a Copy… and opened in another app.

Save a Copy… writes the file as it is into a folder you choose. It is then outside the app, and no longer sealed.

The alert says only “A document expires soon.” It never names the file, because what an alert says shows on the Lock Screen. Nothing about a file goes into an alert or into your other files.

Deleting in Files

Before you decide

When is Stonefold out?

Not yet. It is being finished for iPhone, iPad, and Mac, with no date set.

Is there Stonefold for Android, Windows or the web?

No. Stonefold is made for iPhone, iPad, and Mac only.

What happens if Stonefold Labs goes away?

Stonefold runs on your devices and needs no server of ours, so it keeps working. Your things stay in your files, and Export Everything writes them out in open formats at any time.

Why not use Apple’s own apps?

You may not need to. Apple’s own apps are free and come with your device, and if they do what you need, use them. Stonefold puts the calendar, reminders, notes, a budget, tools, and files in one app with one unlock, for $29.99 once, with no subscription, no AI, and no account. It keeps everything on your device, encrypted, and works offline, and if you turn on iCloud sync, each change is sealed on your device first, with a key only your devices hold. Apple’s own page, “iCloud data security overview” (dated January 5, 2026, read November 24, 2026), says Calendars in iCloud are encrypted “in transit & on server” with the keys held by Apple, whether Advanced Data Protection is on or off, and that Notes and Reminders are end-to-end encrypted only when Advanced Data Protection is on.

Does it connect to my bank?

No. Every transaction is typed by you, and on the Mac you can also paste rows into the budget’s grid. There is no bank connection and no statement import, and nothing is added by itself. Stonefold has no account and we run no server, and nothing leaves your device unless you turn on iCloud sync. A bank connection would send your bank sign-in through a service in the middle.

Can I try it first, or get a refund?

There is no trial: Stonefold costs $29.99 up front, once, for iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Before you decide, this guide shows every part, starting with How it works. Refunds are handled by Apple, not by us, so we can’t promise or give one. You ask Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com; Apple’s page (published September 17, 2026) says a purchase “might be eligible for a refund” and to expect an update in 24 to 48 hours.

Is it open source, and has anyone outside checked it?

Not yet. Today Stonefold is not open source, and no outside firm has reviewed it. For launch, we plan a design paper that sets out each key and where it lives, the passphrase settings, the file format, the seal used for sync, and what it protects against and what it does not. We also plan to open the code for the encrypted store, the encryption, and sync for anyone to read, with a small reader that opens your own saved copies on any computer. From the first release, a dated list of changes will name security fixes as such. One purchase covers your own iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Whether it covers a family is not settled yet.

Any iPhone with iOS 26 or later, any iPad with iPadOS 26 or later, and any Mac with macOS 26 (Tahoe) or later. Apple lists which devices run each on its own support pages. One price covers all three.

Can I share it with my partner?

No. Stonefold keeps one person’s things, and there is no way to give another person access to your calendar, notes, or budget from inside the app. iCloud sync keeps your own devices matching, through your own iCloud. Privacy and your data

Can you see what I keep?

No. We run no server for the app, so we never receive what you keep, and it is stored on your devices, encrypted.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up and nothing to log in to. There are no ads, no tracking, no analytics and no AI.

Does Stonefold work offline?

Yes. Everything you add is saved on your device first, and alerts are set on your device and ring from it. Only iCloud sync uses the internet, and only if you turn it on.

What leaves my device?

With sync off, nothing leaves unless you save it somewhere yourself. With sync on, sealed changes go to your own iCloud, and Apple can see that your Apple Account is syncing, and when. Settings, then Privacy, then What Leaves This Device, says it in plain sentences.

What happens to the documents in Files?

Your files stay on your device, each sealed by itself with a key of its own. The words inside a file are found on your device for search, and nothing is sent anywhere.

How do I take my things out?

In Settings, choose Export Everything. It writes your events, reminders, notes and budget into one new folder in formats other apps can open. Nothing in that folder is encrypted, so Stonefold asks first.

What is left out of an export?

The alarms, timers and cities of Tools aren’t in it, and Recently Deleted is left out. Some marks and settings stay behind too, such as a calendar’s color and which notes are pinned. Save a Copy of the calendar and reminders carries the Tools items.

Sync and your devices

Do I have to use iCloud sync?

No. Sync is a switch in Settings and it starts off. Every part of Stonefold works without it.

How does sync keep my things private?

When you turn it on, each change is sealed on your device first, with a key only your iPhone and Mac hold. It then travels through your own iCloud, and we run no servers.

What does sync cover?

Every section you keep: the Calendar, Reminders, Notes, Budget, Tools and Files. Apple can see that your Apple Account is syncing, and when.

I have a new iPhone or Mac. How do I bring my things over?

If you use iCloud sync, a new device gets the key from one of your devices, with a code you scan or type. You can also use Save a Copy on the old device and Open a Saved Copy on the new one, and your recovery sheet opens what it covers too. A saved copy is separate, so what you add on one device doesn’t appear on the other.

What if I lose my iPhone?

If you use sync, your other iPhone or Mac still holds what you kept. Either way, your recovery sheet or a saved copy opens it on a new device. If you lose your iPhone and Mac and the sheet, nobody can open what you kept, including us.

The lock, passphrases and the recovery sheet

How does the lock work?

With Lock the app on, opening the app asks once for Face ID or Touch ID, or the device’s passcode or password if that fails, and it locks itself when you leave. By default, anyone who can unlock your device can open the app. You can also lock the budget or the notes with a passphrase of your own.

Can I add a passphrase?

You can add one to the budget, the notes, or both, in Settings, then The Lock. The calendar and reminders can’t have one, and Files has none of its own because it opens with the one unlock.

What if I forget a passphrase?

Nobody can get a forgotten passphrase back, not us and not Apple. Your printed recovery sheet is the way back in, and without it nobody can open the budget or notes you locked.

What is the recovery sheet?

It is one printed page with a code for each thing it covers, and it is the way back if a device’s keys are lost or a passphrase is forgotten. Print it and keep it where you would keep a key, because the app never stores a code.

How do I make a recovery sheet?

The app offers it once when you first go in, and again when a file is made later, such as the budget’s. You can also open Settings, then The Lock, and choose Make the Recovery Sheet. A new sheet stops the old one working, for everything the new one covers.

Does Stonefold back up what I keep?

Once a day, when the app is unlocked, what you keep is backed up if something changed, each part encrypted with its own key. The newest 30 daily backups of each are kept, and 10 made with Back Up Now.

Using the app

Can I use only some parts of Stonefold?

Yes. Every part is on when you start. In Settings, go to Sections, and turn off any you don’t use: the Calendar, Reminders, Notes, Budget, Tools or Files. A part that is off is hidden, and everything you put in it is there when you turn it on again.

Can I bring my things from other apps?

Yes, most of it. For the calendar, copy your events once from Apple’s Calendar (Bring Mine Over, in Settings, then Backups and Export), or bring in a calendar file ending in .ics. For reminders, copy your lists once from Apple’s Reminders (Bring Mine Over, in the same place). For notes, bring in a folder of plain text files (Bring In Notes…), and bring in flashcards from Anki or Quizlet files. For Files, send a document from another app to Stonefold, or scan pages with the iPhone’s camera. The budget is typed by you, and on the Mac you can paste rows into its grid; there is no bank or statement import. Nothing stays linked to the other app, and nothing in it is changed.

Can I copy my events from Apple’s Calendar?

Yes, once, when you choose to, from Bring Mine Over, in Settings, then Backups and Export. Titles, places, notes, days and times come over, while alerts, invitations and attachments stay behind. Nothing stays linked, and nothing in Apple’s Calendar is changed.

Can I copy my lists from Apple’s Reminders?

Yes, once, from Bring Mine Over, in Settings, then Backups and Export. Nothing is copied until you choose Bring In, and done reminders, locations, pictures, tags, flags and priorities stay behind. Nothing stays linked, and nothing in Apple’s Reminders is changed.

Does Stonefold have flashcards?

Yes. They are on from the start, and their switch is in Settings, then Sections, then Notes. A deck can hold basic, reversed, type the answer and cloze cards, with pictures if you like, and you can bring in cards from Anki or Quizlet files.
